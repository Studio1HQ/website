# Questions per section

Give each helper its section and the rules in the skill. Findings go in `research/<section>.md` as
bullets: the claim, the figure, the link, the year. Say "not found" rather than guess.

## A. The company

- What the product is, in one plain sentence, and who pays for it.
- Stage and funding: rounds, amounts, dates, lead investors, from the company or a press release.
- Team size now, and the rough trend (careers page, public profiles). Say how you counted.
- Recent launches in the last 12 months: name, date, link.
- Who their developers are: the language, stack or job they have; where they gather; what the
  docs and examples assume they know.

## B. The market

- The category name people search for, and the neighbouring categories.
- Market size with the report, publisher and year. Collect at least two reports; show the range.
- Growth rate, with the period it covers.
- What is changing: new standards, buyer habits, platform shifts, regulation. Each with a link.

## C. Closest competitors (four to six)

- Choose by who the company's buyers compare it with: review sites, "alternatives" searches, the
  company's own comparison pages.
- For each: one line of positioning in their words, who it is for, public pricing (list the plan
  names and limits as published; no pricing means "not public").

## D. How competitors reach developers

For each competitor:

- Blog: posts in the last 90 days, topics, whether they are tutorials or announcements.
- Docs: quality, quickstart time, examples, an AI-friendly docs file if any.
- GitHub: stars, last release, contributor activity, read today.
- Video: channel, upload cadence, typical views.
- Community: Discord, Slack, forum; size if public.
- Events: conferences, meetups, hackathons run or sponsored in the last year.
- Launches: the last three, where they were posted, how they were received.

Then the same facts for the company itself, so the gap is visible.

## E. Region

- Where the company sells (named countries or regions on its site, pricing, currencies, events).
- Which of the competitors, and which other developer-focused companies, are active there.
- Local developer communities, events and publications worth knowing.

## F. The opening (written by the lead)

- Gaps: where the company is behind the competitors in D, with the evidence.
- Closest case studies from `studio1-context`: name each, say why it matches by category, stage or
  problem. Numbers are quoted only from that skill.
- Conflict: any current Studio1 client that competes with the company, or the company itself as a
  current client. Say "none found in `references/clients.md`" when there is none.
