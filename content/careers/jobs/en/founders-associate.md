Studio1 is hiring a Founder's Associate: the person who makes sure everything we promise clients actually ships, on time and to our standard, without the founders chasing it.

Studio1 is a growth and content agency for DevTools companies, at every stage. Our clients ship APIs, SDKs, infrastructure and AI tools. We help developers find, understand and adopt them through technical blogs and tutorials, SEO and AI search, launches on X, developer influencer campaigns, community events and hackathons.

You'll work directly with our two founders and sit between them and everyone else: writers, the community team, client managers and interns. Think of it as running the company's operating system. This is not an assistant role. You won't manage our calendars. You'll manage the work.

## What 100% agency means here

- **You own outcomes, not tasks.** We tell you what good looks like. You choose the route.
- **You don't wait for permission.** A late blog, a vague brief, a report that eats three hours every Friday: if you see it, it's yours to fix.
- **You close loops.** Nothing you touch ends at "I told them." It ends when it's done.
- **You bring bad news early,** along with the fix you'd pick.
- **You push back,** on teammates and on us, when something doesn't make sense.

## What you will do

- **Run the team's week.** Know who is on what, what's due and what's stuck. Set the weekly plan, run check-ins, and unblock people before a deadline slips.
- **Be the founders' right hand.** Turn a voice note or a 10-minute call into a clear plan with owners and dates. Take decisions off our plate when you can, and bring us the ones you can't with a recommendation.
- **Be the quality gate for client work.** Read every deliverable before the client does. Run the code samples, check facts and links, compare it with the brief, and send it back when it isn't good enough.
- **Report without being asked.** Send the founders a weekly note: what shipped, what slipped, what's at risk. Help put together monthly results for clients: rankings, views, AI citations.
- **Automate internal workflows.** Find the manual work that repeats, then kill it with a script, an AI agent, a no-code flow or a feature request for our internal dashboard.
- **Give people context.** Explain to a writer why a client matters, who their developers are and what "good" means for this account. Good briefs prevent most rewrites.
- **Manage interns with them, not over them.** Onboard them, set clear tasks, review their work, give honest feedback and track how they grow.
- **Keep the machine tidy.** Write the SOPs nobody has written yet, keep tools and trackers up to date, and help with hiring when we grow.

## Your first 90 days

- **Days 1 to 30, learn the machine.** Sit in on every client call and team sync, read every client brief, map who owns what, send your first weekly report by week 2, and fix one thing that annoys the team.
- **Days 31 to 60, own the rhythm.** Run the weekly plan and intern check-ins, review every deliverable before it goes out, write the first SOPs, and ship your first automation.
- **Days 61 to 90, run it without us.** The founders stop chasing follow-ups, interns work on a clear plan, you own the monthly reports, and you pitch your first growth experiment.

## What we are looking for

We care more about what you've run than how long you've worked. Usually that means 1 to 3 years at a startup, agency or DevTools company, but a student who ran a big community or hackathon can be a great fit too.

- **You've run things end to end:** a team, a project, an event, a community or a side business. You can show what changed because you were there.
- **You follow through.** People trust you because things you pick up get done.
- **You write clearly.** Your status updates are short. Your briefs can be acted on without a call.
- **You can give hard feedback kindly,** including to people more senior than you.
- **You're organised,** and you build systems instead of relying on memory.

**Developer knowledge matters.** Our clients sell to developers, so you need to think like one:

- You can read code, clone a repo, follow a quickstart and tell when a tutorial's code won't run.
- You understand APIs, SDKs, CLIs and open source, and the basics of AI tooling: LLMs, agents, MCP.
- You know how developers find and choose tools: docs, GitHub, X, Reddit, Hacker News, DEV.

## Bonus points

- You've built something: a side project, an open-source contribution, or a script that saved someone hours.
- You automate with code or no-code: Python or JavaScript, n8n, Zapier, Apps Script, or AI agents like Claude Code.
- You're comfortable with SQL or spreadsheets when a report needs real numbers.
- You know the basics of SEO and AI search: rankings, Search Console, getting cited by AI answers.
- You've grown an account, a community or a newsletter, and you can show the numbers.
- You think in experiments: a hypothesis, a metric, a result, and what you'd do next.

## Where this role can go

The role starts in operations. If you can run growth experiments, it grows from there. Once the agency runs smoothly, you'll get room to test ideas for Studio1's own growth, and later for clients. Experiments that work become yours to run.

- Turn every blog post into a LinkedIn carousel and measure whether reach and inbound go up.
- Test whether comparison pages, like "best agencies for DevTools", bring in booked calls.
- Track how often ChatGPT and Google's AI answers name Studio1, then try to move that number.
- Try new outreach to DevTools teams and find out which message gets replies.

Depending on where you're strongest, this can lead to running operations for the whole agency or leading a growth stream.
