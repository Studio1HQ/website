Studio1 is hiring two Developer Relations Engineers who are builders first, with a GenAI and agentic AI background. You build the demo, then you teach it: your job is to help developers understand, try, and succeed with AI and developer products, for Studio1 and our clients.

This is a developer relations role, **not a software engineering role**, and it needs both halves. You will write real code every week, and the code is there to teach: demos, sample apps, and tutorials that show other developers how to use a product. You will not be building product features, and you won't only be writing or running community either. If you want just one of those, this isn't the right fit.

## What you will do

**Build and ship fast**

- Build and ship demos, sample apps, MCP servers, and reference repos with AI agents and agentic workflows: tool calling, RAG, memory, and multi-agent setups. Get them on GitHub quickly, while the topic is still new.
- Learn new agent frameworks, SDKs, and model APIs fast enough to explain them accurately.

**Teach developers**

- Write tutorials, guides, and blog posts that take a developer from zero to a working result.
- Write and improve documentation: quickstarts, API and SDK guides, READMEs, and integration docs.
- Create videos: demo walkthroughs, YouTube tutorials, and short clips for X and LinkedIn that show what you built.

**Be the developer's voice**

- Test new features and releases as a developer would: follow the docs, find what breaks or confuses, and report it clearly with fixes.
- Answer developer questions on GitHub, Discord, Reddit, and X, and turn repeated questions into better docs or content.
- Bring what developers say back to the product teams we work with.

**Grow the community**

- Help with developer community activities, hackathons, and partnerships with other developer companies, such as joint demos and integrations.
- Review technical content from the team for accuracy.

## What we are looking for

- You are a builder. A GenAI and agentic AI background is a must: you have built AI agent or LLM projects beyond a basic chatbot or API wrapper. Show us the repos.
- You have public developer-facing work: tutorials, blog posts, docs, videos, talks, or community work that other developers used. Code alone isn't enough, and content alone isn't either.
- High agency: you don't wait to be told what to do. You spot what needs doing, figure it out, ship it, and own the result.
- You enjoy explaining things. A developer can follow your tutorial or quickstart without asking you a question.
- You write solid Python or TypeScript and are comfortable with LLM APIs, tool calling, RAG, and structured outputs.
- You notice the details when testing a product: the missing step, the wrong default, the error message that doesn't help.
- 1+ year in developer relations, developer advocacy, or developer education, or as an engineer who also writes, teaches, or runs a developer community. Strong public work counts as much as job titles.
- A plus: a technical blog or YouTube channel, open-source contributions, community moderation, or experience working with partners.

## Why join

You will build real GenAI and agentic AI projects and be the person developers learn them from: through your demos, tutorials, docs, and answers. You will build your own name in the developer community while doing it.

## Role details

This is a full-time remote role for India-based candidates. You will work closely with the founders and the entire team.

## How to apply

Apply with the form on this page. Have these ready:

1. Your resume and GitHub profile.
2. Developer-facing work you made: tutorials, docs, blog posts, videos, talks, or community work.
3. Links to two or three GenAI or agentic AI projects you built, and anything you made to explain them.
4. A few lines on why you want developer relations, and not a software engineering role.
5. One short doc: pick an AI developer tool, try its quickstart and one newer feature, and tell us what broke or confused you and how you'd fix the docs.

We usually respond within 1 week if there is a fit.

**We ask for repos and public work because this role needs both: someone who builds, and someone developers learn from.**
