Studio1 partnered with **Permit.io**, a developer-first authorization platform, to design and execute a technical content and distribution program that delivered two outcomes simultaneously: practical developer education for high-intent authorization topics, and increased **qualified developer traffic** to Permit.io's product education and implementation pages.

The engagement didn't stop at content. It extended into **distribution and launch support**: syndicating work across developer platforms and coordinating execution around Permit.io's **Product Hunt** launch, where the product won **Product of the Day**.

## About Permit.io

[**Permit.io**](https://www.permit.io) is a developer-first authorization platform that helps engineering teams implement and manage permissions: **RBAC, ABAC, and policy-based access control**: across modern application stacks.

For Permit.io, content is not a marketing add-on; it's a **primary acquisition channel**. Developers evaluating an authorization layer search for concrete implementation answers: *"RBAC in Supabase"*, *"authorization vs authentication"*, not brand messaging. Winning those searches meant pairing strong SEO fundamentals with guides practical enough to double as onboarding.

## The challenge: content that had to rank and explain implementation

Permit.io needed content built for two fronts at once:

* **Growth-oriented content** that increases discoverability, earns backlinks, and captures early-stage developer interest.
* **Deep technical implementation guides** that rank for high-intent queries and convert readers into product users through real, hands-on workflows.

In parallel, the team needed help activating **distribution channels** like Dev.to, and coordinating execution around their **Product Hunt launch**: a separate motion that still had to draw on the same content engine.

## Our role

Studio1 operated as Permit.io's embedded content engine, covering the strategy and execution both tracks required:

* **SEO-driven topic strategy**: selecting topics for discoverability and link potential, not just coverage
* **Technical guide authorship**: writing full implementation walkthroughs mapped to real Permit.io use cases
* **Ghostwriting**: producing content in Permit.io's technical voice, reviewed for accuracy
* **Distribution ops**: syndicating and repurposing content across Dev.to and Medium
* **Launch execution support**: coordinating content and activity around the Product Hunt launch

### 2. Technical implementation guides (rank + activate)

Alongside growth content, we built **step-by-step technical guides** mapped directly to Permit.io use cases and real developer workflows.

The flagship example: **"How to Implement RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) in Supabase."**

<Frame>
  <img src="https://mintcdn.com/studio1/NFIzOkeC-xlQv6Ju/images/permit-blogs.png?fit=max&auto=format&n=NFIzOkeC-xlQv6Ju&q=85&s=fff4263a1c3e992ac26c95c3ec4fa670" alt="Permit.io RBAC in Supabase technical implementation guide" />

Why it performs:

* It clearly distinguishes **authentication vs. authorization**, then makes the case for RBAC as a scalable foundation.
* It walks through a complete RBAC setup using Permit.io's own concepts: **roles, resources, actions, tenants, and policies**.
* It shows real enforcement using **Supabase Edge Functions**, including a permission-check endpoint powered by Permit's `check` flow.

The result is content that doesn't just rank: it helps developers understand implementation paths while they're reading it.

The engine worked because ranking and adoption were never treated as separate problems. Distribution then stretched every asset's lifecycle past search, onto Dev.to, onto Medium, and into the Product Hunt launch, so the same body of work kept paying out across channels instead of decaying after a single publish.
