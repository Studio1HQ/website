We started working with **Jozu** in **October 2024**, supporting developer advocacy, open-source community work, technical content, events, and partner collaboration. The work helps developers understand **KitOps** and Jozu's AI/ML infrastructure story through practical education, public event presence, and ecosystem conversations.

## About Jozu

[**Jozu**](https://jozu.com/) works on infrastructure for AI/ML teams, including **KitOps**, an open-source project for packaging AI/ML projects into OCI-compatible artifacts. KitOps helps teams package models, datasets, code, configuration, and related assets into a portable unit that can move through existing registry and deployment workflows.

That category needs developer trust. Builders do not adopt packaging, governance, and deployment workflows because of slogans. They need practical examples, credible technical writing, community support, and people who can explain the work in front of developers.

## The challenge: make KitOps and Jozu easier to understand in the ecosystem

Jozu operates where several audiences meet: ML engineers, DevOps teams, platform engineers, Kubernetes users, and AI infrastructure teams. The work needed to connect those worlds clearly.

The developer-facing work needed to:

* make KitOps easier to understand through practical technical education
* support community conversations around AI/ML packaging and open source
* represent Jozu in relevant developer spaces and events
* help turn ecosystem opportunities into useful public-facing collaboration

The goal was steady developer-facing momentum, not isolated content pieces.

## Our role

Studio1 supported Jozu across developer advocacy, technical content, event presence, community support, and ecosystem collaboration.

The work stayed practical: useful examples, credible developer conversations, and public-facing support where it helped Jozu and the community.

## What we delivered

### 1. Event and booth representation for Jozu and KitOps

Studio1 represented Jozu at public developer events, including **KubeCon + CloudNativeCon India 2025** in Hyderabad and **DevFest Pune 2025**.

At these events, Studio1 was the main on-ground presence for Jozu's booth conversations: speaking with developers, explaining the product and open-source project, answering questions around AI/ML packaging and Kubernetes workflows, and bringing the right context back to the team.

Event work created direct feedback from developers: what they understood quickly, what needed more explanation, and where docs or demos could become clearer.

### 2. Technical content and blog support

Studio1 supported Jozu with technical blogs and developer education content around KitOps, AI/ML packaging, Kubernetes workflows, model operations, and ecosystem use cases.

The focus was to make complex infrastructure topics easier for developers and platform teams to understand, while keeping the writing technical, useful, and aligned with Jozu's product narrative.

### 3. Community support and the Jozu Champions program

Studio1 also supported the community side of Jozu and KitOps: developer conversations, contributor-facing context, social posts, and the **Jozu Champions** program.

The focus was technical relevance: keep the community work useful for builders, not just promotional.

### 4. Talks, meetups, and local developer education

Studio1 supported meetup opportunities and talks where KitOps could be explained in Kubernetes, DevOps, AI/ML, and open-source community spaces.

### 5. Ecosystem and partner collaboration

Studio1 helped support collaboration work with relevant teams in the cloud native and AI infrastructure ecosystem. One public example is the collaboration around **KitOps, Cog, and KAITO**, where Jozu and partner authors published a CNCF piece on standardizing AI/ML workflows on Kubernetes.

## The results

* Jozu and KitOps represented in direct developer conversations at public events
* Technical blog and social content motion supported across planning, drafting, editing, and distribution
* KitOps community support strengthened through developer conversations and Champions program work
* Talks and meetup opportunities supported around AI/ML packaging, Kubernetes, and open-source workflows
* Ecosystem collaboration supported with relevant cloud native and AI infrastructure teams

## Why it works

Jozu is growing its developer presence through a connected mix of technical content, events, community support, talks, and ecosystem collaboration.

That gives developers multiple ways to understand KitOps: reading the ideas, seeing practical examples, asking questions in person, and finding the project through the cloud native ecosystem around it.
