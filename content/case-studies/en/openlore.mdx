Studio1 ran a **one-month DevRel engagement** with **OpenLore** in September and October 2026. We started by making the product easier to understand, then put it in front of developers through talks, hackathons, articles, and daily social distribution, and fed what we heard back to the founders.

In **just one month**, OpenLore saw **12x GitHub star growth**, from about 135 to 1,700+, reached **100K+ impressions** across platforms, and trended **#3 in Go on Trendshift**. It is one of our biggest wins to date.

## About OpenLore

<Frame>
  <img src="/case-studies/openlore/openlore-homepage.webp" alt="OpenLore homepage: Build the knowledge your agents rely on" />
</Frame>

[**OpenLore**](https://openlore.sh), built by LIX Ltd, is a **shared docs server that coding agents can safely read and edit together**. Teams and their agents get one current source of context instead of scattered Markdown files that drift apart.

It is new infrastructure in a crowded AI tooling space, so adoption depends on clarity: developers need to see the problem it solves, not just read a feature list.

## The challenge

OpenLore needed to:

* Make the product **easy to understand and try** on first visit
* Get in front of **developers building with coding agents**
* Keep attention going long enough to turn it into **steady GitHub growth**
* Learn what developers actually wanted from the product

## Our role

* **DX audit** of the README, install, and docs
* **Positioning** and launch guidance
* **Talks and hackathons** that put OpenLore in builders' hands
* **Articles and technical content** around the context drift problem
* **Daily social distribution** across X, LinkedIn, daily.dev, and Reddit
* **Developer feedback loops** that turned questions into feature requests
* **Demo projects** that put OpenLore to work in real agent workflows

## What we delivered

### 1. Making OpenLore easy to understand

Before driving traffic, we reviewed OpenLore the way a new developer meets it and shared prioritised recommendations, along with the **"docs server for coding agents"** positioning. The founders acted on much of it during the engagement. The clearest example is the GitHub README, which now explains the product in a line and a diagram.

### 2. Talks and hackathons

Arindam introduced OpenLore to developers in the [**"Ship It Together"**](https://arindamm.dev/slides/ship-it-together) talk and at hackathons, showing how teams and their agents can share one source of knowledge. Stars jumped after each session.

The conversation kept going after the events. Attendees and other developers produced **200+ LinkedIn posts and comments** about OpenLore, carrying it well beyond the rooms we spoke in.

### 3. Articles and daily social distribution

To keep the momentum from the events, we published articles and posted about OpenLore **every day** across X, LinkedIn, daily.dev, and Reddit. Each piece led with a problem developers recognise, agents working from different versions of the truth, and showed OpenLore as the fix.

Together this drove **100K+ impressions** in one month. The biggest contributors:

* A **daily.dev** post with **50K impressions**, all organic
* An [**X Article**](https://x.com/Arindam_1729/status/2112892916159111359) on giving every coding agent the same memory, with **24K views**
* A launch video on X, LinkedIn posts, and Reddit threads in agent and LLM developer communities

### 4. Developer feedback and feature requests

Talking to developers also gave OpenLore direction. After the sessions and posts, developers came back with questions and **feature requests** on bringing in existing docs, access control, and team workflows. We passed these straight to the founders, so the product could improve based on what users were asking for.

### 5. Demo projects and new ideas

We also built with OpenLore ourselves. We implemented it in projects like an [**OpenCrewBot integration**](https://github.com/Studio1-OSS/OpenCrewBot/pull/1) and [**RelayDesk**](https://github.com/shivaylamba/relaydesk), a support-to-engineering app, so developers could see it working in real agent workflows. Building these, and taking feedback on them, gave us first-hand input for the founders and sparked new product ideas.

* **100K+ impressions** across daily.dev, X, LinkedIn, and Reddit
* **#3 trending in Go** on [Trendshift](https://trendshift.io/repositories/85262)
* **200+ LinkedIn posts and comments** from developers after the sessions
* **DX improvements shipped** by the founders during the engagement
* **Feature requests** from real users, fed back into the product

## Why it worked

Each part fed the next. The DX audit made OpenLore easier to understand, talks and hackathons put it in front of builders, and daily distribution kept the conversation going long after each event. What developers asked for then flowed back to the founders.

Running all of it in parallel is what made one month enough. For a new category of agent tooling, clarity, reach, and a steady feedback loop are what turn curiosity into stars.
