Studio1 worked with **InsForge**, the open-source backend for AI coding agents, on content, launch distribution, and developer community. The work started a few weeks before InsForge's first Launch Week, and it carried on through the months after it.

That launch became the turning point for the company. The announcement passed **1.5M views on X**, InsForge reached **#1 on Product Hunt and #1 on GitHub Trending**, and weeks later it joined **Y Combinator**. Over the same period its GitHub stars grew **nearly 10x**, and **3,000+ of those stars** came directly from activities we designed.

## At a glance

- **Client:** InsForge, the open-source backend platform that gives coding agents a database, auth, storage, functions, and a model gateway
- **Services:** launch content and distribution, technical content and SEO, AI search visibility, developer video, community distribution, hackathons and workshops
- **Launch:** a Launch Week announcement with **1.5M+ views** on X, **#1 on Product Hunt**, and **#1 on GitHub Trending**
- **GitHub:** **1.3K stars** when we started, **12.7K** by the end of our work, **13.3K** today
- **Our direct contribution:** **3,000+ stars** and **160+ signups** from activities we designed and ran
- **Search and AI:** page-one Google rankings, a source in a **Google AI Overview**, and a **ChatGPT citation**

## About InsForge

[**InsForge**](https://insforge.dev) is an open-source backend built for AI coding agents. Instead of a developer wiring up a database, auth, storage, and functions by hand, an agent like Claude Code or Cursor can set up and operate the whole backend itself, through a layer designed for agents to understand.

The team is in Y Combinator's Spring 2026 batch, where the company is now listed as InstaBox.

## The challenge: standing out in a crowded category

Backends for developers are a busy market with large, well-known players. InsForge had a sharp idea, a backend that agents can run end to end, but it needed developers to see it, try it, and care, all at once.

The team picked a date for its first Launch Week and gave itself about a month to get ready. That meant:

* Launch content and demos that show the product working, not just announce it
* Distribution across the places agent builders already spend time
* Technical content that keeps bringing developers in after launch week ends
* A way to reach the next generation of builders, at hackathons and campus events

## Our approach

A few ideas shaped the work:

* **Launches are won before launch day.** The content, demos, and posts were ready and lined up before the first announcement, so every day of the week had something new to show.
* **Show the product doing the work.** For a backend built for agents, the most convincing proof is an agent building a real app on it, so demos and tutorials led with that.
* **Meet builders where they already are.** X, Reddit, Product Hunt, GitHub, YouTube, and hackathons each got content shaped for that audience, not the same link pasted everywhere.
* **Write for search and for AI answers.** Tutorials and deep dives target the questions agent builders actually ask, so they keep working long after launch week, in Google and in AI assistants.

## The outcomes

### 1. A Launch Week that reached 1.5M+ developers

We helped prepare and distribute InsForge's first Launch Week: launch content, demo videos and visuals, posts from our engineers on X, community distribution on Reddit, and support for the Product Hunt launch and the GitHub Trending push.

The main announcement passed **1.5M views on X**. In InsForge's own account of the week, it also reached **#1 on Product Hunt** and **#1 on GitHub Trending**, gaining **3K+ GitHub stars in one week**.

### 3. GitHub stars grew nearly 10x

For an open-source product, GitHub stars are a public measure of developer interest. When we started, the InsForge repo had about **1.3K stars**, after months of slow growth. Launch Week turned the curve, and the steady flow of content and community work afterwards kept it climbing. By the end of our work it had **12.7K stars**, and it has since passed **13.3K**.

Part of that growth is ours to count directly. Across four activities we designed and ran for InsForge, participants added **3,000+ stars** to the repo, with the largest one bringing in more than half of them.

### 4. Technical content that ranks on Google

Launch attention fades in days; search traffic doesn't. We wrote build-along tutorials and deep dives for the InsForge blog, each showing an agent building something real on InsForge: semantic chat with pgvector, a multimodal RAG system with Gemini embeddings, an AI analytics dashboard, a real-time social app, and explainers on MCP design and the AI agent stack.

Topics were picked from what agent builders were already searching for. Several posts now rank on **page one of Google**, including **#2** for the AI agent stack (MCP, skills, and plugins), and page one for multimodal RAG with Gemini embeddings and for WebMCP's execution model.

### 5. Cited by Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT

More developers now get their answers from AI, so we wrote for that too: clear definitions, structured explanations, and concrete examples that an AI answer can quote.

It shows. Google's **AI Overview** for "AI agent stack: MCP, skills, plugins" lists our InsForge post as a source, alongside Microsoft, and the same post ranks **#2** in the results below it. When we asked **ChatGPT** how context-first MCP design reduces agent failures on backend tasks, its answer cited our InsForge post.

### 6. Video, community, and hackathons

Beyond the blog, we made developer videos on YouTube featuring InsForge, and kept it in front of agent builders on X and Reddit between launches.

We also took InsForge to student builders, setting up partnerships with student hackathons and running hands-on workshops where participants built with it themselves.

Across this work, the activities we designed were built to convert, not just to be seen. Together they brought in **3,000+ GitHub stars**, and several doubled as signup campaigns: one push in March alone added **160+ new signups**.

## Why it worked

The launch was planned as a week of proof, not a single announcement. Every day had something new to show, and every channel got content made for it, so the attention built on itself instead of spiking once.

Just as important, the work didn't stop when the launch did. Tutorials kept bringing developers in from Google and AI assistants, and hackathons put InsForge in the hands of new builders. That is why the star count kept climbing long after launch week.
