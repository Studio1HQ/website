**The short answer:** Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the work of getting your brand mentioned, cited and described accurately in answers written by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Copilot and Google AI Mode. The research that named it found that adding quotations, statistics and cited sources raised a page's visibility in generated answers by up to 40%, while keyword stuffing hurt. In practice, GEO means letting AI search crawlers in, publishing passages worth quoting, earning mentions on the sites engines cite most, and tracking a fixed set of prompts every month.

This guide covers what GEO is, what the research actually proves, how each engine picks its sources, a nine-step playbook, what changes for developer tools, and how to measure it. Every number links to the page it came from. Its sister guide, [the complete guide to AEO](/blog/answer-engine-optimization-guide), covers how to become the answer in Google's AI Overviews, AI Mode and featured snippets.

## What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

Generative engine optimization is optimizing your content and your presence across the web so that AI systems that write answers mention you, cite you and describe you correctly. Ahrefs defines it as getting your brand [mentioned, cited, and accurately represented](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-vs-geo/) in AI-generated answers.

The term comes from a research paper. In November 2023, researchers from IIT Delhi, Princeton and two independent researchers in Seattle published [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) on arXiv. It was accepted to KDD 2024. The paper treated ChatGPT-style answer engines as a new kind of search, built a benchmark of 10,000 queries, and tested which content changes made a source more visible in the generated answer.

Google's view is blunter. Its May 2026 guide to [optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says optimizing for AI search is "still SEO", and Google's announcement of the guide included a section on [mythbusting common "AEO/GEO" misconceptions](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing). Both views are right. The foundation is SEO. What's new is that an answer can name you without anyone clicking, and that much of what an engine says about you comes from pages you don't own.

| | SEO | AEO | GEO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question it answers | Does my page rank? | Is my page the answer shown? | Does the AI mention, cite and describe us correctly? |
| Main engines | Google, Bing | AI Overviews, AI Mode, featured snippets, Copilot | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, AI Mode |
| Where the work happens | Your site | Your site, page by page | Your site and the sites engines trust |
| Proof it worked | Rankings, clicks | AI impressions, citations, clicks | Mention rate, citations, share of voice, conversions |

For the page-level work of becoming the answer, read [our AEO guide](/blog/answer-engine-optimization-guide). This guide focuses on the rest: the research, the engines, and the off-site work.

**Key pointers:**

* Keep SEO as the base. Google's own guide says GEO without indexable, useful pages is a myth.
* Treat GEO as a brand question as much as a page question: what does the AI say about you, and where did it learn it?

## Why does GEO matter in 2027?

Because the audience moved and the clicks didn't follow. Generative engines now reach billions of people, but they send relatively few visitors, so the answer itself is where your brand is won or lost.

**The reach:**

* Google's AI Overviews has [over 2.5 billion monthly active users](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/sundar-pichai-io-2026/), and AI Mode passed 1 billion in its first year (May 2026).
* The Gemini app reached [one billion users](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q3-2026/) by October 2026.
* OpenAI's own research put ChatGPT at [700 million weekly users](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/a253471f-8260-40c6-a2cc-aa93fe9f142e/economic-research-chatgpt-usage-paper.pdf) in July 2025, with information-seeking up from 14% to 24% of use in a year. In November 2026, an OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch that [1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-acquires-odessia-travel-startup-created-by-sonder-founder/).
* In the US, [42% of adults use chatbots to search for information](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/), and six in ten read AI search summaries.

**The shift between engines:** Similarweb found generative AI sites averaged [9.5 billion visits a month](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/gen-ai-stats/) between June 2025 and May 2026, up 70% on the year before. ChatGPT's share of those visits fell from about 76% to about 53%, while Gemini rose from under 9% to about 27% and Claude from about 2% to about 9%. Optimizing for ChatGPT alone is already a mistake.

**The traffic reality:** Across 118,671 sites in October 2026, AI assistants sent [0.46% of traffic, against 24.74% from Google](https://ahrefs.com/benchmarks/ai-vs-search). The visits are few but valuable. On Ahrefs' own site, AI search brought [0.5% of traffic but 12.1% of signups](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-traffic-conversions-ahrefs/) in mid-2025. That's one site, but it matches what we hear from devtool teams: people who arrive from an AI answer have already been told what you do.

**Key pointers:**

* Measure GEO by what the answers say first and by visits second. The visits are a small slice of traffic.
* Track at least ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Claude. Engine share is moving fast.

## What does the GEO research actually prove?

The original paper is the only controlled study of GEO tactics most guides cite, and most guides get its numbers wrong. Here's what it tested and found.

**The setup:** The researchers built GEO-bench, [10,000 queries from nine sources](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3), each paired with the top five Google results. They rewrote one source at a time using nine methods, then measured how visible that source was in the generated answer. Visibility had two measures. Position-Adjusted Word Count counts the words tied to a source's citation, weighted down the later they appear. Subjective Impression has an AI judge score relevance, influence, uniqueness, position and click likelihood.

**The results:** The best methods beat the baseline by [41% on Position-Adjusted Word Count and 28% on Subjective Impression](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3). Scores from the paper's Table 1, where the unchanged baseline scored 19.5:

| Method | What it means | Position-Adjusted Word Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quotation Addition | Add quotes from credible people | 27.8 |
| Statistics Addition | Replace vague claims with numbers | 25.9 |
| Fluency Optimization | Make the text read more smoothly | 25.1 |
| Cite Sources | Add citations to credible sources | 24.9 |
| Baseline | No change | 19.5 |
| Keyword Stuffing | Repeat the query's keywords | 17.8 |

Three findings matter most:

* **Keyword stuffing doesn't work.** The paper found such methods offer [little to no improvement](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3), and stuffing scored below the unchanged baseline.
* **It held on a real engine.** On Perplexity, Quotation Addition gave a [22% improvement](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3) over the baseline.
* **Smaller sites gain most.** Citing sources raised visibility for sites ranked fifth in Google by [115.1%](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3), while sites ranked first lost 30.3% on average. GEO rewards the best-supported passage, not the biggest domain.

The limits matter too. GEO-bench is a benchmark, run on 2023-era models, with each query limited to five sources. Today's engines search more widely and many models have changed since. Read the paper as evidence of direction: evidence-dense, quotable text beats keyword-dense text. Don't read it as a promise of 40% more citations.

**Key pointers:**

* Rewrite your key pages so each claim carries a number, a named source or a quote from someone credible.
* Delete keyword repetition. In the only controlled test, it made pages less visible.

## How do generative engines choose their sources?

Every engine follows roughly the same path: read the prompt, search, pick the passages that answer cleanly, write, and cite some of what it used.

![Six steps a generative engine takes: read the prompt, fan out into searches, retrieve pages, pick passages, write the answer, and cite sources, with the pick-passages step highlighted](/blog/uploads/generative-engine-optimization-guide/how-a-generative-engine-answers.webp "From one prompt to an answer with sources. Your page has to survive every step.")

**They search with their own crawlers and indexes.** Each engine documents its bots, and blocking the wrong one removes you from its answers:

| Engine | Crawler that powers answers | What blocking it does | Training crawler (separate) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | `OAI-SearchBot` | [Not shown in ChatGPT search answers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) | `GPTBot` |
| Perplexity | `PerplexityBot` | [Not surfaced or linked](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) in Perplexity results | Not used for training, per Perplexity |
| Claude | `Claude-SearchBot` | [May reduce visibility](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler) in Claude's search results | `ClaudeBot` |
| Google AI Overviews, AI Mode | `Googlebot` (normal Search) | Out of Search, including AI features | `Google-Extended` |
| Copilot | `Bingbot` (Bing's index) | Out of Bing and Copilot | Not separately documented |

Both OpenAI and Perplexity also have user-triggered fetchers (`ChatGPT-User`, `Perplexity-User`) that act when a person asks about a specific page.

**They fan out.** Google says AI Mode breaks a question into subtopics and [issues a multitude of queries](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-search-ai-mode-update/) at once, and Deep Search can run hundreds. ChatGPT does this less than people assume: Ahrefs found only [14.4% of ChatGPT responses contained fan-out searches](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt/), about 1.6 each, and the most common word added was the current year. Engines look for fresh, dated answers.

**They lean on a few kinds of sites.** In Ahrefs' November 2026 ranking of [the sites ChatGPT cites most](https://ahrefs.com/blog/most-cited-domains-in-chatgpt/), Wikipedia led at 10.1%, ahead of Reddit at 6.2% and Consumer Reports at 4.4%. In [Google AI Mode](https://ahrefs.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai-mode/), Reddit (18.1%) and YouTube (17.0%) came right after Google's own pages. Mentions matter more than links: in a 2025 study of 75,000 brands, web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at [0.664, against 0.218 for backlinks](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/). And assistants prefer recent pages, citing content [25.7% fresher](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/) than organic results, with ChatGPT the most freshness-hungry.

**We saw this ourselves.** We asked Perplexity "how to implement RBAC in Supabase" in November 2026. Its answer drew on 25 sources: ten pages from Supabase's own docs and blog, five GitHub repositories and ten YouTube videos. Not one third-party vendor blog made the list, including guides that rank on Google's first page for the same question.

![Perplexity answer to how to implement RBAC in Supabase, with YouTube tutorial thumbnails at the top and a sources panel listing Supabase documentation pages](/blog/uploads/generative-engine-optimization-guide/perplexity-rbac-supabase.webp "Perplexity's answer to \"how to implement RBAC in Supabase\": official docs, GitHub and YouTube supplied all 25 sources (November 2026).")

**Answers change every time.** Reporting on a SparkToro and Gumshoe study, Search Engine Journal found there was [less than a 1-in-100 chance](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/) that an AI tool would return the same list of brands twice, and the same order less than 0.1% of the time. Ranking in one answer means nothing. Being mentioned across many runs does.

**Key pointers:**

* Check your robots.txt against the table above. Blocking `OAI-SearchBot` to stop training removes you from ChatGPT search.
* For how-to questions, the official docs, GitHub and YouTube supply the answer. Make sure your product shows up in all three.

## How to do GEO: a 9-step playbook

This is the order we run GEO in for clients. The first three steps make you findable, the next three make you quotable, and the last three make you trusted and measured.

### 1. Build a prompt set

Write 20 to 30 prompts real buyers would ask, across four types: the category ("best open-source vector database"), the problem ("how do I add memory to an AI agent"), comparisons ("X vs Y") and your brand ("what is X, is X good"). Use full sentences. Google says [AI Mode queries are three times longer](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q3-2026/) than classic searches, and chat prompts are longer still.

### 2. Let the answer crawlers in

Search crawlers and training crawlers are different bots with different opt-outs. A starting robots.txt that keeps you in AI answers looks like this:

```txt
# Crawlers that put you in AI answers: allow them
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

# Training crawlers are a separate decision.
# To opt out of training only, disallow these instead:
# User-agent: GPTBot
# User-agent: ClaudeBot
# User-agent: Google-Extended
# Disallow: /
```

Check the Search Console [generative AI control](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/) too. Sites that opt out get no traffic or impressions from Google's AI features. OpenAI says robots.txt changes take [about 24 hours](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) to apply.

### 3. Be in the indexes they search

Engines answer from search indexes, so the basics come first: pages indexed in Google and Bing, rendered as HTML text, listed in a sitemap with honest `lastmod` dates, and submitted through IndexNow when they change.

### 4. Write passages worth quoting

Apply the paper's winning methods to your key pages. Replace vague claims with numbers, cite the source next to each number, and quote named people with real credentials. Put each fact in its own short paragraph that still makes sense if it's the only thing an engine lifts.

### 5. Stay the freshest credible source

Assistants prefer fresh pages, and ChatGPT's added search terms are most often the current year. Update your key pages when the facts change, show a visible updated date, and say which year your numbers are from. Don't change the date without changing the page.

### 6. Earn mentions where engines look

Wikipedia, Reddit, YouTube and review sites supply a large share of citations, and mentions track visibility more closely than links. Answer real questions in the subreddits your buyers read, publish video walkthroughs, get included in honest third-party comparisons, and keep your GitHub and review profiles current. For developer communities, see our guide to [Reddit marketing for developer tools](/blog/reddit-marketing-for-developer-tools).

### 7. Make your entity consistent

Use the same name, one-line description and category on your site, docs, GitHub, LinkedIn, directories and press pages. When an engine gets a fact about you wrong, find where the wrong fact lives and fix it at the source.

### 8. Publish honest comparison pages, not page farms

"X vs Y" and "best tool for Z" prompts decide shortlists, so publish comparisons with real numbers and trade-offs. Don't mass-produce pages for every possible sub-question. Google's guide calls that [scaled content abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

### 9. Measure monthly and fix the biggest gap

Run the prompt set every month, several times each, and record mentions, citations and what's said about you. Fix the single biggest gap first, then measure again. The next section has the tools.

**Key pointers:**

* Do steps 2 and 3 this week. They take an hour and remove the most common reasons you're invisible.
* Spend at least half your GEO time off your own site, on step 6.

## How does GEO work for developer tools?

For developer tools, engines answer from docs, code and video, and our Perplexity test shows how concentrated that is. Each kind of question has a source the engines prefer:

| Developer prompt | Where engines get the answer | What to publish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "How do I [task] with [tool]?" | Official docs, GitHub, YouTube | A task-based quickstart, a runnable example repo, a short video |
| "Best [category] for [use case]" | Third-party comparisons, Reddit, review sites | Honest comparisons on your site, and mentions on others' |
| "[Tool] vs [tool]" | Comparison posts, Reddit threads | Your own comparison with a table and benchmarks |
| "What is [tool]?" | Your homepage, docs, Wikipedia, GitHub README | One consistent description everywhere |
| "[Error message]" | GitHub issues, docs, Stack Overflow | Troubleshooting pages with the exact error text |

So for a devtool, GEO mostly means three things. Write docs that answer tasks, not just list APIs, as we explain in [developer docs as your best AEO asset](/blog/developer-docs-as-aeo-asset). Make your GitHub presence complete, starting with a [README that converts](/blog/github-readme-best-practices). And put real walkthroughs on [YouTube](/blog/youtube-for-developer-tools). For a step-by-step plan, see [how to get your tool recommended by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google](/blog/how-to-get-your-tool-recommended-by-chatgpt-perplexity-google). An [llms.txt file](/blog/llms-txt-explained-developer-tools-guide) won't change what Google says about you, but coding agents that read docs directly can use it.

**Key pointers:**

* For every core task in your product, check that docs, an example repo and a video exist. Those are the three sources engines reached for in our test.
* Write the "what is [tool]" sentence once and use it word for word everywhere.

## How do you measure GEO?

You measure GEO by what the engines say, not by rank: how often you're mentioned, how often you're cited, how your share compares with competitors, and whether the description is accurate. Then you check the visits and conversions those answers send.

| Tool | What it measures | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search Console generative AI report | How often your URLs [appeared in Google's AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports), by page, country, device and date | Impressions only, no clicks or queries |
| Bing AI Performance | [Citations, cited pages and grounding queries](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) across Copilot, plus [Citation Share](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare/) since June 2026 | Preview, sampled queries |
| Ahrefs Brand Radar | Mentions, citations and AI share of voice across [558 million+ prompts](https://ahrefs.com/brand-radar) on six AI platforms | Ahrefs' prompts, not your buyers' |
| Peec AI | Visibility, position, sentiment and sources, with [prompts run every 24 hours](https://peec.ai/) | Paid, your prompts only |
| Profound | Citations across [ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini and more](https://www.tryprofound.com/), plus crawler analytics | Paid, enterprise-focused |
| Otterly.AI | Mentions, citations and [share of AI voice](https://otterly.ai/) across seven engines | Paid |
| Semrush AI Toolkit | An [AI Visibility Score](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1493-ai-toolkit), share of voice and sentiment | Semrush's prompt set |
| Your analytics | Visits and signups from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com, claude.ai and copilot.microsoft.com | Some AI visits arrive with no referrer |

If you'd rather start without paying for a tool, our two-hour spreadsheet method in [how to measure your devtool's visibility in AI answers](/blog/how-to-measure-devtool-visibility-in-ai-answers) covers the prompt set, the runs and the scoring.

**Key pointers:**

* Report four numbers monthly: mention rate, citation rate, share of voice against your top three competitors, and AI-referred signups.
* Record what the engines say about you, word for word. Accuracy problems are GEO problems.

## What GEO mistakes should you avoid?

Most GEO mistakes either block the engines, chase a trick, or measure the wrong thing:

* **Blocking the search crawler instead of the training crawler.** `OAI-SearchBot` and `GPTBot` do different jobs. So do `Claude-SearchBot` and `ClaudeBot`.
* **Keyword stuffing.** It scored below the baseline in the GEO paper.
* **Page farms.** Mass-producing pages for every fan-out question is what Google calls scaled content abuse.
* **Treating llms.txt as GEO.** Google says it isn't needed and that Search ignores AI text files.
* **Chasing one answer.** Brand lists change on almost every run. Track rates across many runs.
* **Ignoring off-site mentions.** If the only page praising you is yours, engines notice.
* **Unsourced claims.** Citing sources was one of the methods that raised visibility. A claim without a source is a claim an engine can't trust.

**Key pointers:**

* Audit robots.txt and the Search Console generative AI control once a quarter.
* Before publishing a GEO page, check it adds a number, a source or a first-hand result nobody else has.

## What changes in 2027?

Generative engines are turning into agents that browse, compare and buy. These are announced, not predicted:

* **Background agents.** Google announced [information agents](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/sundar-pichai-io-2026/) at I/O 2026, and Chrome's [auto browse](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/gemini-3-auto-browse/) handles multi-step tasks for subscribers.
* **Agentic checkout.** Google's [Universal Commerce Protocol](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/agentic-commerce-ai-tools-protocol-retailers-platforms/) is an open standard for checkout inside AI Mode and the Gemini app, built with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart.
* **Agents read your site differently.** Google's guide says [browser agents may access your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) to complete tasks, reading it through screenshots, the page structure and the accessibility tree.

For 2027, that adds three items to the playbook. Make your site usable by an agent: semantic HTML, labelled buttons, and pricing and limits stated as text. Spread your effort across engines as Gemini and Claude grow. And keep the evidence fresh, because the engines check dates and add the current year to their searches.

**Key pointers:**

* Test your signup and pricing pages with an accessibility checker. Agents rely on the same structure screen readers do.
* Put the year on your data and refresh it. "2027" will be the word engines add to their searches next year.

## FAQ

### What is GEO in simple terms?

GEO is making sure AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude mention your brand, cite your pages and describe you correctly when people ask about your category. It covers your own content and the sites those tools learn from.

### Is GEO replacing SEO?

No. Google says optimizing for its generative AI features is still SEO, and every engine answers from a search index. GEO adds off-site mentions, AI-specific crawlers and new ways to measure what answers say.

### What's the difference between GEO and AEO?

AEO aims to be the answer shown on answer surfaces such as AI Overviews, AI Mode and featured snippets, mostly through how each page is structured. GEO aims to be mentioned and cited inside generated replies across engines, which depends heavily on your presence on other sites. Our [AEO guide](/blog/answer-engine-optimization-guide) covers the page-level side.

### Does llms.txt help GEO?

Not with Google. Google's guide says you don't need llms.txt and that its Search ignores AI text files. Coding agents may read it when they browse your docs, so it's cheap to add for developer tools, but it won't get you recommended.

### Should I block GPTBot?

Blocking `GPTBot` opts you out of OpenAI's model training. It doesn't remove you from ChatGPT search, which uses `OAI-SearchBot`. If you want to appear in ChatGPT answers, never block `OAI-SearchBot`.

### How do I check if ChatGPT mentions my brand?

Run a fixed set of 20 to 30 buyer prompts in ChatGPT several times each month and record whether you're mentioned, cited and described correctly. Tools like Ahrefs Brand Radar, Peec AI, Profound, Otterly and Semrush's AI Toolkit automate this on their own prompt sets.

### How long does GEO take to work?

Crawler and robots.txt fixes apply within a day or so. Content changes show up once engines recrawl and re-index, usually within weeks. Off-site mentions take months to build. Judge progress by the trend in your monthly prompt runs over a quarter.

### Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT answers?

Not in the answers themselves. ChatGPT started [testing ads in the US](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/09/chatgpt-rolls-out-ads/) in February 2026, and OpenAI says ads do not influence the answers ChatGPT gives. Google's [ads in AI Mode](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ads-in-ai-mode-global/) appear as sponsored placements. Organic mentions still have to be earned.
