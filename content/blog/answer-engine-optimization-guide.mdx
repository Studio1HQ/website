**The short answer:** Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the work of making your page the source an answer engine uses when someone asks a question. The engines are Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, Bing Copilot, ChatGPT and Perplexity. Google says there is no special trick: it is still SEO. What changes is the page. Ask one question per section, answer it in the first two sentences, back it with proof, and keep the page crawlable and eligible for snippets. Then measure it in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

This guide covers what AEO is in 2027, which answer engines matter, what Google and Microsoft have actually said, a nine-step playbook, copy-paste answer templates, and how to measure the results. Every number links to the page it came from. Its sister guide, [the complete guide to GEO](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-guide), covers how to get cited and recommended inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude.

## What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?

Answer engine optimization is optimizing content to be chosen as the direct answer to a question, instead of one of ten blue links. Kalicube, a brand SEO firm, [says it coined the term in 2017](https://kalicube.com/entity/answer-engine-optimization/), when the targets were featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes and voice assistants.

The target has moved. Ahrefs tracked a million US desktop results in 2025 and found [featured snippets fell from 15.41% to 5.53% of results](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-serp-features-have-evolved-in-the-ai-era) between January and June, while AI Overviews rose from 3.93% to 27.43%. In July 2026, Google said it had [brought AI Overviews and AI Mode together](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/) into one Search experience. The answer at the top of Google is now written by a model.

Google's own position matters here. Its May 2026 guide to [optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says optimizing for AI search is "still SEO", and it lists "AEO/GEO hacks" as something to avoid. That doesn't make AEO meaningless. It means AEO is SEO plus two things: pages shaped as answers, and a habit of checking what the answer engines actually say.

| | SEO | AEO | GEO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Rank a page in search results | Be the answer an engine shows | Be cited or recommended in a generated reply |
| Main surfaces | Google and Bing results | AI Overviews, AI Mode, featured snippets, Copilot, voice | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Copilot |
| Unit that wins | A page | A passage that answers one question | A brand, a source or a passage |
| Proof it worked | Rankings, clicks | AI impressions, citations, clicks | Mentions and citations across prompts |

The three overlap. Most of the work that wins an AI Overview also helps you get cited in ChatGPT, and none of it works on a page Google can't index.

**Key pointers:**

* Treat AEO as a layer on top of SEO, not a replacement. Google's own guide says so.
* Judge each section by one test: if an engine lifted only its first two sentences, would they answer the question?

## What do Google and Microsoft say about AEO?

Google and Microsoft have both published guidance, and they don't say quite the same thing.

**Google's position:**

* **Nothing special is required.** Google's page on [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. A page needs to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet.
* **No llms.txt, special schema or chunking.** Google's [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says structured data isn't required and there is no special schema for AI. It also says there's no need to break content into tiny pieces.
* **Query fan-out is real.** The same guide describes the model running a set of related searches to gather more information. That is why a page can be cited for a question it doesn't rank for.
* **Snippet controls apply.** Google's [robots meta tag docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag) say `nosnippet` also keeps a page out of AI Overviews and AI Mode as a direct input. `max-snippet` caps how much is used, and `data-nosnippet` hides one element.
* **There's an opt-out, and it costs traffic.** Since August 31, 2026, Search Console has a [site-wide generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024?hl=en). Google says sites that opt out [won't get traffic or impressions](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/) from its generative AI features. It doesn't affect AI training, which `Google-Extended` controls.

**What's gone:** Google limited FAQ rich results to government and health sites and [retired HowTo results in 2023](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes). Then FAQ rich results [stopped appearing on May 7, 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage). FAQ markup no longer earns a special result on Google.

**Microsoft is more specific.** A Bing product manager wrote that assistants [lift Q&A pairs word for word](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers), recommended one- or two-sentence answers that address the question directly, and suggested lists and tables for how-to and comparison content. The same post warns that AI systems may not render content hidden in tabs or accordions. Bing also ties [IndexNow to freshness in AI answers](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview).

None of this conflicts. Google won't reward formatting tricks, and Bing says plainly that clean question-and-answer structure gets lifted. The page that satisfies both is a page written for a person in a hurry.

**Key pointers:**

* Check every important page for `nosnippet`, `max-snippet:0` and the Search Console opt-out. Any of them can keep you out of AI answers.
* Remove FAQ schema only if it's broken. It's harmless, but it no longer earns a rich result on Google.

## How do answer engines choose which page to quote?

They run searches, pick passages that answer cleanly, and favour sources that already rank for the question or for one of its sub-questions. Five findings explain most of it.

**1. Questions trigger answers.** Pew's tracking of real users found AI summaries on [60% of searches that start with who, what, when or why](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), and 53% of searches with ten or more words, against 18% overall. Ahrefs measured AI Overviews on [57.9% of question queries](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-overviews/).

**2. Ranking still helps, but less each year.** In March 2026, Ahrefs found only [37.9% of AI Overview citations ranked in the top 10](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10) for the query, down from about 76% in July 2025. Another 31% came from beyond the top 100. Ahrefs links the drop to fan-out: the model searches for sub-questions and cites pages that answer those.

**3. Covering the follow-up questions pays.** In data reported by Ahrefs, pages that ranked across the fan-out sub-queries were [161% more likely to be cited](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-overviews/). That's a correlation, but it fits how fan-out works.

**4. Length doesn't matter. Structure might.** The same Ahrefs analysis found almost no link between word count and being cited. AirOps studied pages ChatGPT cites and found [68.7% used sequential headings](https://www.airops.com/report/structuring-content-for-llms), against 23.9% of Google's top results. Again a correlation, not proof.

**5. Schema didn't move citations.** Ahrefs tracked [1,885 pages that added schema](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/) against 4,000 that didn't, and found no major citation uplift on any platform after 30 days.

Here is what that looks like in practice. Studio1 helped Tensorlake publish an explainer on [TOON vs JSON](https://www.tensorlake.ai/blog/toon-vs-json) when the format started trending. When we searched "toon vs json" on Google in the US in November 2026, the AI Overview cited Tensorlake inline and listed the article in its sources, next to a Reddit thread.

![Google AI Overview for the query toon vs json citing Tensorlake inline and listing Tensorlake's TOON vs JSON article in its sources next to a Reddit thread](/blog/uploads/answer-engine-optimization-guide/google-ai-overview-toon-vs-json.webp "Google's AI Overview for \"toon vs json\" cites the Tensorlake explainer Studio1 helped publish (US, November 2026).")

The article wasn't written for AI Overviews. It answered a new question early, defined both formats plainly, and showed a comparison with real numbers. That is most of AEO.

**Key pointers:**

* Target questions, especially long ones. That's where answer boxes appear most.
* For each page, list the three follow-up questions a reader would ask next, and answer them on the page.

## How to do AEO: a 9-step playbook

These are the steps we run for every page that should become an answer. They follow the order that matters: first be eligible, then be the clearest answer, then be the best-supported one.

![Six parts of an answer-first section: the question as a heading, a short answer, a list or table, a source, the follow-up questions, and a visible date, with the short answer highlighted](/blog/uploads/answer-engine-optimization-guide/answer-first-section.webp "The anatomy of a section built to be quoted.")

### 1. Collect the questions people actually ask

Start with evidence, not guesses. Pull question-shaped queries from Search Console (filter for who, what, how, why, vs, best). Add People Also Ask boxes, support tickets, sales-call notes and the threads in your community. Then type the same questions into AI Mode and ChatGPT and note the follow-ups they suggest. Keep the asker's exact words.

### 2. Give each question its own heading

One question per H2 or H3, phrased the way people ask it. A page about one topic can answer six questions. A section that answers two questions answers neither well.

### 3. Answer in the first two sentences

Microsoft's guidance is [one- to two-sentence answers](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers) that address the question directly. Our house rule is 40 to 60 words that still make sense if they're the only thing quoted. Name the subject in the answer ("AEO is…", not "It is…"), because a lifted passage loses its heading.

### 4. Use the shapes engines lift

Steps go in a numbered list. Comparisons go in a table. Definitions are one sentence. Keep the answer in plain HTML text that loads with the page, not inside tabs, accordions or images, which Bing says [AI systems may not render](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers).

### 5. Back every answer with proof

Put the evidence right after the answer: a number and where it came from, a date, a screenshot, a result you got yourself. Google's [people-first content checklist](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether a page offers original information, research or analysis. First-hand proof is the one thing a model can't produce from other pages.

### 6. Answer the follow-ups

Fan-out means the engine is also searching for the next three questions. Answer them on the page, or link to the page that does. Pages that rank across those sub-queries were [161% more likely to be cited](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-overviews/).

### 7. Keep the page eligible

Make sure the page is indexed, server-rendered, free of `nosnippet`, and not caught by the Search Console generative AI opt-out. Use structured data where Google still shows a rich result for it, such as product and review snippets. Don't add it expecting AI citations. Show a visible updated date.

### 8. Refresh, then tell the engines

Update numbers when newer data lands, change the date only when the page changed, and keep your sitemap's `lastmod` honest. Submit changed URLs through [IndexNow](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) for Bing and the engines that use its index.

### 9. Be the answer where you can't host it

Some answers come from places you don't own: Reddit threads, YouTube videos, Wikipedia, review sites, GitHub. Show up there with real help, not links. This is where AEO hands off to GEO, covered in [our GEO guide](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-guide).

**Key pointers:**

* Run steps 1 to 4 on your top ten pages before writing anything new. Restructuring is faster than publishing.
* Make the first two sentences under each heading quotable on their own, with the subject named.

## What should an answer look like? Four templates

Copy these and fill in the brackets. Each starts with the direct answer and ends with something only you can add.

**Definition ("What is X?")**

> [X] is [category] that [does what] for [whom]. [One sentence on how it works or how it differs from the thing people confuse it with]. [One number or example, with its source].

**How-to ("How do I X?")**

> To [do X], [first step], [second step], then [final step]. It takes about [time] and needs [prerequisites].
> 1. [Step with the exact command or setting]
> 2. [Step]
> 3. [Step, and what the reader should see]

**Comparison ("X vs Y")**

> [X] is better for [situation], and [Y] is better for [situation]. The main difference is [one difference that decides it].
> | | X | Y |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | [Criterion] | [Fact] | [Fact] |

**Recommendation ("What's the best X for Y?")**

> For [specific use case], [option] is the strongest choice because [reason with evidence]. [Option 2] fits [other case]. Avoid [option] if [condition].

The fourth template is the hardest to win, because engines prefer to cite sources other than the vendor for "best" questions. Your own page helps, but third-party reviews and comparisons carry more weight. That's GEO's territory.

**Key pointers:**

* Lead with the answer, then the reason, then the proof, in that order, every time.
* Never leave a template's placeholder evidence vague. "Fast" loses to "200 ms p95 in our benchmark".

## How do you measure AEO?

You measure AEO with four sources: Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, a fixed set of prompts you check by hand or with a tracker, and your own analytics.

| Tool | What it shows | What it doesn't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search Console Performance report | Clicks from AI features, counted [inside normal "Web" search traffic](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) | Which clicks came from AI |
| Search Console generative AI report | [How often your URLs appeared](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) in AI features, by page, country, device and date (all sites since Aug 31, 2026) | Clicks, or AI Overviews vs AI Mode separately |
| Bing AI Performance (preview) | [Citations, cited pages and a sample of grounding queries](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) across Copilot and Bing AI summaries | Clicks |
| Third-party trackers | Mentions and citations for prompts the vendor runs, like [Ahrefs Brand Radar](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/11064852-what-is-brand-radar-and-how-to-use-it) | Real user conversations |
| A manual prompt panel | What the engines say about your category today | Anything statistically solid on its own |

For the manual panel, use the method in [how to measure your devtool's visibility in AI answers](/blog/how-to-measure-devtool-visibility-in-ai-answers): fixed prompts, several runs, monthly.

Then watch clicks, because answers take them. With an AI summary on the page, Pew's panel clicked a result on [8% of visits, against 15% without](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), and only 1% clicked a link inside the summary. Ahrefs found an AI Overview now correlates with a [58% lower click-through rate](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/) for the top result. There's an upside: across 5.47 million queries, Seer Interactive found brands cited in the AI Overview averaged a [2.07% organic click-through rate against 0.94%](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update) for brands that weren't cited. That's a correlation, not proof the citation caused it. Being the answer is how you keep the clicks that are left.

**Key pointers:**

* Turn on both reports now: Search Console's generative AI report and Bing's AI Performance report.
* Report AI impressions and cited pages next to clicks every month. Clicks alone will look like decline.

## What AEO mistakes should you avoid?

Most AEO mistakes come from chasing a trick instead of answering the question better. These are the ones we see most:

* **Adding FAQ schema for a rich result.** Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in May 2026.
* **Padding pages.** Word count has almost no link to being cited. A 300-word page that answers cleanly beats a 3,000-word one that doesn't.
* **Hiding answers.** Answers inside tabs, accordions, images or client-rendered widgets may never be read.
* **Blocking yourself.** A stray `nosnippet`, a `max-snippet:0` or the Search Console opt-out removes you from AI answers.
* **Measuring once.** One prompt on one day tells you nothing. Answers change between runs.
* **Writing the vendor's answer to a "best" question.** Engines lean on third-party sources there. Earn the mention elsewhere.
* **Ignoring the click.** Give people a reason to visit after the answer: code, a calculator, a template, data they can't get in the summary.

**Key pointers:**

* Audit for the three self-blocks (`nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, the opt-out) once a quarter.
* For every page you optimize, write down why someone would still click after reading the answer.

## What changes in 2027?

Answer engines are becoming agents that act, and ads are moving into the answers. These are announced, not forecasts:

* **Agents in Search.** Google announced [information agents](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) at I/O 2026, and in October its CEO said agents will [operate as a unifying layer](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q3-2026/) across its products.
* **Ads in the answer.** Ads in AI Mode are [expanding to 10 more countries](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ads-in-ai-mode-global/), and ChatGPT began [testing ads in the US](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/09/chatgpt-rolls-out-ads/) in February 2026.
* **Search is still growing.** Google says its year-over-year query growth is [the highest in five years](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q3-2026/).

One forecast worth knowing: Semrush projected that AI search could [send more visitors than traditional search by early 2028](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-search-seo-traffic-study/), with AI visitors converting at 4.4 times the rate. It studied marketing and SEO topics only, so treat it as a direction, not a date.

For 2027, that points to three priorities. Write answers an agent can act on: clear pricing, clear steps, clear limits. Make product and docs pages complete enough that an agent doesn't need to guess. And expect the organic answer to share the screen with an ad, which makes being the cited source worth more, not less.

**Key pointers:**

* Make pricing, limits and setup steps explicit on the page. Agents can't act on "contact us".
* Plan for fewer, better clicks. Measure conversions from AI referrals separately.

## FAQ

### Is AEO different from SEO?

AEO is part of SEO, not a separate discipline. Google says optimizing for AI search is still SEO and that no special markup is needed. AEO adds a focus on question-shaped sections, short direct answers and measuring AI impressions and citations, not just rankings.

### Is AEO the same as GEO?

They overlap but aim at different things. AEO aims to be the answer shown on an answer surface such as an AI Overview or featured snippet. GEO aims to be cited or recommended inside generated replies from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude. See [our GEO guide](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-guide).

### Does schema markup help AEO?

Not for AI citations, based on current evidence. Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages that added schema and found no major citation lift on any platform. Google says no special schema is needed for AI features, and FAQ rich results left Google in May 2026. Keep schema for the rich results it still earns.

### How long should an answer be?

One or two sentences, then the detail. Microsoft recommends one- to two-sentence answers that address the question directly. We aim for 40 to 60 words that make sense on their own, followed by a list, table or proof.

### Do I need llms.txt for AEO?

No. Google says you don't need llms.txt for its AI features. It can still help coding agents that read your docs directly, which is why we cover it in [our llms.txt guide](/blog/llms-txt-explained-developer-tools-guide).

### How do I know if my site appears in AI Overviews?

Use the generative AI report in Google Search Console, which shows how often your URLs appeared in AI features by page, country, device and date. It has been available to all sites since August 31, 2026. It shows impressions, not clicks.

### Can I opt out of AI Overviews?

Yes. Search Console has a site-wide control for Google's generative AI features. Google says sites that opt out won't get traffic or impressions from those features. The `nosnippet` rule also keeps a page out of AI Overviews and AI Mode.

### How long does AEO take to work?

Changes to pages that already rank can show up within weeks, once Google recrawls them. New pages take as long as normal SEO, because they need to be indexed and trusted first. Check the same prompts and reports monthly, and judge the trend over a quarter.
