**The short answer:** Yes. Comparison and alternatives pages reach people at the moment they choose a tool, and AI assistants lean on them: in an [Ahrefs study of ChatGPT answers across 750 prompts](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/), "best X" lists made up 43.8% of the page types cited. With developers, they convert only when they read as fair. Put the verdict first, say where the other tool wins, date and source every claim, show the same task in both tools, and end with migration steps that work.

## Which comparison pages should a devtool publish?

Four page types cover most comparison searches, each at a different point in the decision. They are one part of a wider [technical content marketing](/blog/technical-content-marketing-for-devtools) program.

| Page type | Example search | Where the searcher is | What the page must have | Next step to offer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| X vs Y | "supabase vs firebase" | Down to two named tools | Verdict, when-to-pick table, same task in both | Quickstart |
| X alternatives | "v0 alternative" | Doubting a tool, building a shortlist | Why people leave X, 3 to 8 options, who each fits | Migration guide |
| Best X for a use case | "best feature flag tool for startups" | Has a job, no shortlist yet | Criteria up front, a reason per pick, proof you tried them | Use-case tutorial |
| Migration guide | "migrate firebase auth to supabase" | Decided, or pricing the switch | Concept map, commands, code before and after, rollback | Signup |

To see which ones you already get impressions for, open Search Console, go to Performance, add a Query filter and choose **Custom** in its dropdown, the regex option Google [added in 2021](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/04/performance-report-data-filtering). Paste a pattern like this, with your competitors' names added:

```text
vs|versus|alternative|compare|comparison|migrate|switch from|provider-x
```

Queries with impressions and no page built for them are your first briefs.

**Key pointers:**

- Build one page per decision, not one per keyword variation. Google says pages for query variations made to manipulate rankings or AI answers [violate its scaled content abuse policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).
- Start with the competitors your sales calls name, and pair every alternatives page with a migration guide.

## Why developers distrust comparison pages, and how to earn trust back

Developers have read too many comparison pages that are ads. Ahrefs found that in 169 of 250 "best X software" searches (67.6%), Google showed a list where the author's company ranked itself first. Agency owner Wil Reynolds, quoted in the same study: "Any marketer worth a damn knows listing yourself at the top of a list hosted on your site doesn't build trust with buyers."

Developers also check sources. Stack Overflow's [2026 survey results](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/) say "to trust what the AI gives requires source attribution (93%)." Hold your page to that bar with four habits.

### Say where the other tool wins

Tailscale's [WireGuard comparison](https://tailscale.com/compare/wireguard) says: "Using WireGuard directly offers better performance than using Tailscale. Tailscale does more than WireGuard, so that will always be true." PostHog's [Mixpanel comparison](https://posthog.com/blog/posthog-vs-mixpanel) ends its "when to choose" section with: "Prefer a polished UI optimized for product managers and growth teams, with strong warehouse-native analytics? Mixpanel is a solid choice."

![Tailscale's WireGuard comparison page, Performance section, stating that using WireGuard directly offers better performance than Tailscale](/blog/uploads/comparison-alternatives-pages-for-developers/tailscale-wireguard-performance.webp "Tailscale says plainly where the other option wins, then explains why.")

### Date every claim

Google's [guidance on page dates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates) says to show a visible, labeled date (for example "Last updated") that matches your structured data. Good pages still miss this: Supabase's [Firebase comparison](https://supabase.com/alternatives/supabase-vs-firebase) has seven feature tables and a 2025-08-20 publish date, but no updated date, so readers can't tell whether its pricing is current.

### Link a source for every competitor fact

On Studio1's comparison pages, every fact about the other company comes from its own website, listed under Sources with a checked date. If a service isn't listed there, the table says "Not listed on their site" rather than guess. Google's [guidance on reviews](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews) asks for "evidence such as visuals, audio, or other links of your own experience."

### Show the code and the method

Prisma's [TypeORM comparison](https://www.prisma.io/docs/orm/more/comparisons/prisma-and-typeorm) runs the same operations through both APIs, such as Prisma's `findUnique` next to TypeORM's `findOne`, and credits TypeORM for having "done a great job" on type safety. PlanetScale's [benchmarks page](https://planetscale.com/benchmarks) names its workloads, explains how to reproduce them, admits one setup gave rivals double the CPUs ("an unfair advantage to them"), and lists an email for corrections.

**Key pointers:**

- Write at least one "Pick [competitor] if..." line that the competitor's own team would agree with.
- Give the feature table a Source column and a "Last checked" date.
- For any performance claim, publish the machine, versions, date and code, or drop the claim.

## Before and after: a biased verdict and a credible one

The same verdict paragraph, written two ways. Acme and Provider X are made-up job queue products.

**Before:**

> Acme is the fastest, most reliable job queue for modern teams. Unlike Provider X, which is outdated and painful to scale, Acme delivers blazing performance and a developer experience that's second to none. Teams that switch never look back. Start free today.

**After:**

> Pick Acme if you want hosted queues with retries, cron schedules and a dashboard, and you don't want to run Redis yourself. Pick Provider X if you already run Redis, need to self-host, or want an open-source library with no per-job fee. In our test on one 4 vCPU machine, Provider X enqueued jobs faster, so if raw throughput on your own hardware decides it, Provider X wins (method and code below). Prices and limits were checked on both pricing pages on 7 November 2026.

What changed: each tool got a situation, the competitor got a real win with no "but", the speed claim points to a method, prices carry a date, and the sign-up push is gone.

**Key pointers:**

- Delete every adjective in your verdict that a competitor could also claim about itself.
- If the verdict has no sentence that starts "Pick [competitor] if", it isn't finished.

## A comparison page template you can copy

**Template: an X vs Y page.** Swap in your product and the competitor.

```markdown
Title: Acme vs Provider X: [the decision this page settles]
Byline: [author, role] · Last checked: 2026-11-07 · We make Acme

## The short answer
[2 or 3 sentences: who should pick Acme, who should pick Provider X,
and the one tradeoff that decides it.]

## When to pick each
| Pick Acme if you...          | Pick Provider X if you...           |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| [a situation, in full words] | [a situation where Provider X wins] |
| [a situation]                | [a situation]                       |

## Side by side (checked 2026-11-07)
| Feature                    | Acme  | Provider X | Source               |
| -------------------------- | ----- | ---------- | -------------------- |
| Pricing model              | [...] | [...]      | [their pricing page] |
| Free tier limits           | [...] | [...]      | [link]               |
| Self-hosting               | [...] | [...]      | [link]               |
| SDK languages              | [...] | [...]      | [link]               |
| [what buyers ask most]     | [...] | [...]      | [link]               |

## The same task in both
[One real task, both code samples, with package versions.]

## Where Provider X is the better choice
[Plain sentences. No "but".]

## Migrating from Provider X
1. Export: [command]
2. Map concepts: [Provider X term -> Acme term]
3. Change the code: [before and after snippet]
4. Run both side by side, verify, cut over: [how to roll back]

## Questions from sales calls
[4 to 6 questions, each with a 2 to 4 sentence answer]

## Sources and method
[Every competitor fact links to their own page. How you tested.
An email address for corrections.]
```

### Title formulas for each page type

- **X vs Y:** "[Acme] vs [Provider X]: [Category] Compared", as in "Studio1 vs Draft.dev: Developer Marketing Agencies Compared".
- **Alternatives:** "[N] [Provider X] Alternatives for [Use Case], Checked [Month Year]"
- **Best for a use case:** "Best [Category] for [Job]: [N] Options We Tested"
- **Migration:** "Migrate from [Provider X] to [Acme]: Step by Step, with Code"

### A worked example: Studio1's own comparison pages

[Studio1 vs Draft.dev](/compare/studio1-vs-draft-dev) and [Studio1 vs Infrasity](/compare/studio1-vs-infrasity) come from one data file: a short answer, a feature table, "Choose Studio1 if" and "Choose [competitor] if" lists, FAQs, sources and a `checked` date that also feeds `dateModified` in the structured data. The sources box opens with "We're Studio1, so read this with that in mind." Our [agency list](/best-developer-marketing-agencies) says "We're Studio1, so we list ourselves first," and shows how the rest were picked. Agencies need no migration section. A devtool page does.

### Migration steps: show the commands

Supabase's [Firebase Auth migration guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/migrating-to-supabase/firebase-auth) gives the exact commands. Between the clone and the export, you add a `supabase-service.json` file and a Firebase private key saved as `firebase-service.json`:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/supabase-community/firebase-to-supabase.git
node firestoreusers2json.js [<filename.json>] [<batch_size>]
node import_users.js <path_to_json_file> [<batch_size>]
```

It also names its limit: verifying existing Firebase passwords on first login needs a middleware server, documented in the repo. Name your hard part the same way.

**Key pointers:**

- Write each when-to-pick row as a full sentence that names both tools, so it makes sense when quoted alone.
- Run every migration command on a clean machine before you publish, and print the package versions.

## How do you keep comparison pages current?

The lists ChatGPT cites tend to be fresh: in the Ahrefs study, published December 2025, 79.1% of cited "best" lists "were last updated in 2025, and 26% were last updated in the past two months alone."

**Review schedule: copy this into your content calendar.**

| What changes | Where to check | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitor pricing and plan limits | Their pricing page | Monthly |
| Competitor features | Their changelog or release notes | Monthly |
| Your own pricing, limits and features | Your release checklist, with "update comparison pages" as a line | Every release |
| Code samples and package versions | Run the snippets, in CI or by hand | Every release |
| What searchers and AI answers say | The regex filter and a fixed prompt test | Monthly |

Change "Last checked" only after you re-check. Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) flags "changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed." Add a one-line change log at the bottom ("2026-11-07: Provider X raised its free tier").

**Key pointers:**

- Create a monthly calendar event listing every competitor's pricing URL, owned by one named person.
- Keep comparison data in one file, as Studio1 does, so the table, the visible date and the structured data change together.

## How do AI assistants use comparison pages?

Recommendation questions are comparison questions, as our [guide to getting recommended by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google](/blog/how-to-get-your-tool-recommended-by-chatgpt-perplexity-google) explains. Google's [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) describes query fan-out as "A set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model to request more information." A question like "which job queue for Node?" can branch into vs, alternatives and pricing searches.

In the Ahrefs software prompts, a brand's own landing pages were cited in 37.2% of answers and its own "best" list in 34%. Ahrefs still holds back: its comparison posts will "make up less than half a percent of our total blog content," and "We won't always put ourselves in the top spot."

Assistants quote passages, not pages, so the short answer and each when-to-pick row must stand alone. If your migration guides live in the docs, our post on [docs as an AEO asset](/blog/developer-docs-as-aeo-asset) covers how to structure them for citation.

**Key pointers:**

- Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode "[Provider X] alternatives for [use case]" and "[Acme] vs [Provider X]", and record which pages are cited.
- When an answer repeats an outdated claim, fix your own version of that fact first. Our [AI visibility audit](/blog/how-to-measure-devtool-visibility-in-ai-answers) walks through the full monthly check.

## Link comparison pages from your docs and tutorials

Developers reach migration guides through your docs, so link both ways. PostHog's comparison answers "Can I migrate my data from Mixpanel to PostHog?" with a link to its migration guide, and Supabase's migration guide lists "Supabase vs Firebase" under Resources.

**Link map: where to add links this week.**

| From | Where on that page | Example link text | To |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quickstart | One line under the title | "Coming from Provider X? Map your concepts first." | Migration guide |
| Concept docs | A short "Differences from Provider X" note | "How Acme retries differ from Provider X" | Matching comparison section |
| Tutorials | The first mention of a competitor's term | "Using Provider X's repeatable jobs? Here's the Acme version." | That migration step |
| Comparison page | Right under the verdict | "Migrate from Provider X in four steps" | Migration guide |

If you keep an llms.txt file, list these pages in it, one line each.

**Key pointers:**

- Link to heading anchors (`/compare/acme-vs-provider-x#migrating`), not only the top of the page.
- Use link text that names both tools, never "learn more".

## How to measure comparison pages

Rank shows a page was found, not that it worked. As our post on [content ROI beyond pageviews](/blog/developer-tool-content-roi-beyond-pageviews) puts it, a comparison page should show whether high-intent visitors explored pricing or sales pages after reading.

| Metric | Where to find it | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signups and demo requests that started on the page | Conversion events by landing page | Whether the page does its job |
| Clicks to the migration guide and pricing | Click events on those links | How many readers reached the next step |
| Queries the page shows for | Search Console, filtered to the page | Whether the intent matches the content |
| AI citations and referrals | The monthly prompt test, plus chatgpt.com and perplexity.ai in referrer reports | Whether assistants use the page |
| Competitors buyers name | An optional "What else did you look at?" field on the demo form | Which page to write next |

Review quarterly: impressions without clicks means the title misses the decision, clicks without signups means a weak verdict or next step, and signups without impressions means the page needs more links.

**Key pointers:**

- Tag the three links that matter on each page (verdict, migration guide, pricing) before you publish.
- Add the "What else did you look at?" field to your demo form this week.

## FAQ

### Do comparison pages work for SaaS SEO?

Yes. They reach people already choosing a tool, and Ahrefs found "best X" lists made up 43.8% of the page types ChatGPT cited across 750 prompts. With developers, they convert only when they are fair, dated and sourced.

### Should I rank my own product first on a "best X" list?

You can, if you say so and give reasons. Ahrefs found self-ranked lists in 169 of 250 "best X software" searches, though Ahrefs says it won't always rank itself first. Studio1's agency list ranks Studio1 first and says so at the top.

### How often should I update a comparison page?

Check competitor pricing and changelogs monthly, and your own claims every release. Change the visible date only after you re-check, since Google flags dates changed just to look fresh.

### Should comparison pages live in the docs or on the marketing site?

Put the comparison on the marketing site and the migration guide in the docs, then link them both ways. Supabase keeps its Firebase comparison under /alternatives, and its Firebase Auth migration guide in the docs links back to it.

### Can I write a comparison against a tool I haven't used?

Not well. Google's reviews guidance asks for evidence of your own experience, so sign up, run their quickstart and keep screenshots. If a fact comes only from their docs, say so and link it.
