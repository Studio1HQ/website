**The short answer:** A product launch needs three videos, not one. Post a hero film of 1 to 3 minutes from the founder that names the problem, shows the product working and gives people a reason to act this week. Follow it with 3 to 5 short clips that each show one workflow, in the thread and over the next few days. Then cut a 15 to 30 second version with the product on screen from the first frame for ads and LinkedIn.

We helped [InsForge](/case-studies/insforge) prepare and distribute its first Launch Week, whose announcement passed 1.5M views on X. For this guide we also analyzed **2,701 product launch posts on X** from January 7 to November 18, 2026, taken from a public directory of X launches that records each post's views, likes, bookmarks, video length and follower count. As in our [guide to viral launches on X](/blog/viral-launch-on-x), we compare each launch with what a typical launch from an account that size gets, so "1.42x" means 42% more views than that.

This post is about the videos. For how to script a single demo, read our [demo video script guide](/blog/how-to-script-a-technical-demo-video). For the launch-day run sheet and the creator wave, read the [viral launch guide](/blog/viral-launch-on-x).

## Why one launch video is not enough

A launch has three jobs: get attention, explain the product, and bring people back who were interested but didn't act. One video can't do all three, and it only gets one window to try. X's open-source ranking code drops posts older than [48 hours](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/params/config.rs) from the feeds it ranks, so a single film has two days at most.

InsForge's Launch Week shows the pattern. The hero announcement passed **1.59M views**. The feature clips that followed each day, posted from the company account, drew between about **1,000 and 9,600 views** each. The hero did its job; the clips needed their own push from people with their own audiences, not just the company account.

![Diagram of the launch video suite: a 1 to 3 minute hero film, 3 to 5 thread clips of 20 to 45 seconds, and a 15 to 30 second short cut](/blog/uploads/product-launch-video/launch-video-suite.webp "The launch video suite: three videos with three different jobs.")

The three videos, and the job each one does:

1. **The hero film** starts the launch. It carries the story, the proof and the offer.
2. **The thread clips** keep it going. Each one shows one workflow, so people can see the product doing their job.
3. **The short cut** follows up. It reaches people who saw the launch, or should have, through ads and LinkedIn.

**Key pointers:**

- Plan three videos with three jobs before you film anything.
- A post has about 48 hours of ranking on X. Spread the clips and the short cut across the week after it.
- Clips posted only from the company account rarely travel. Give each one to people who will post it.

## Write the brief before you film

Most weak launch videos are weak before the camera is on. The script tries to say everything, so it says nothing. Write a one-page brief first, and only film what it needs.

Fill in these six lines, one sentence each:

```text
LAUNCH VIDEO BRIEF

Angle:        The one claim the launch makes. A result or a comparison,
              not the product name.
Viewer:       Who this is for, in the words they use about themselves.
Proof shot:   The single moment on screen that makes the claim believable.
              It must appear in the first 10 seconds.
The change:   What the viewer stops doing, or does in minutes instead of days.
Act now:      The reason to try it this week: credits, a deadline, a contest.
Who posts:    The founder's account first. List who quotes it in the first hour.
```

Two rules make the brief work.

- **Lead with the claim, not the name.** In our [viral launch analysis](/blog/viral-launch-on-x), first lines that named the product reached 0.85x a typical launch, and comparisons reached 1.64x. The same goes for a video's first seconds.
- **Show the proof early.** LinkedIn's own guidance for video ads is to put [the most compelling content in the first 10 seconds](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a424737). On X, people decide even faster. If the proof shot needs a minute of setup, find a smaller one.

**Key pointers:**

- Write the angle as a claim or a comparison. The product name can come second.
- Pick one proof shot and put it in the first 10 seconds of every video.
- Decide who posts and who quotes before you record, not on launch day.

## Video 1: the hero film (1 to 3 minutes, from the founder)

The hero film is the launch post's video. It should run long enough to tell a story and show real proof, and it should come from the founder.

The data favors longer films than most teams expect. Among 1,889 launches with video from accounts under 100K followers, films of **2 to 3 minutes reached 2.83x** a typical launch for the account size. Films of 1 to 2 minutes reached 1.42x. The weakest length was **30 to 60 seconds, at 0.78x**: long enough to lose the scroll, too short to tell a story.

![Bar chart of launch video length vs reach: 2 to 3 minute films reached 2.83x a typical launch, 30 to 60 second videos 0.78x](/blog/uploads/product-launch-video/launch-video-length.webp "Launch video length and reach, 1,889 X launches with video from accounts under 100K followers.")

There is a trade-off. Longer films reached more people, but fewer of them liked what they saw: 2 to 3 minute films got 1.7 likes per 1,000 views, against 3.7 for 30 to 60 second videos. So judge the hero film by signups and bookmarks, not likes.

The clearest example this year is [Jev](https://x.com/CompleteSkeptic/status/2099925682726002904), TypeSafe AI's model. Its founder posted a **2 minute 56 second film** from an account with about 1,500 followers. The post and the film start from his question, why superhuman chat models haven't led to AGI, rather than from a product tour. It reached **40.2M views**, about 227x a typical launch for that account size. Five days later, a short [second post](https://x.com/typesafeai/status/2101786156572823624) ("Jev is now available to everyone. No waitlist.") drew another 3.1M.

![Jev launch post on X from the founder's account: a 2:56 film with the founder on camera, 40.2M views](/blog/uploads/product-launch-video/jev-launch-film.webp "Jev's launch film: the founder on camera, 2 minutes 56 seconds, 40.2M views.")

A hero film that works usually runs in this order:

1. **0:00 to 0:10.** The angle and the proof shot. No logo intro.
2. **0:10 to 0:40.** The problem, told by the founder in plain words.
3. **0:40 to 2:00.** The product doing the job. Let one run play without cuts, so viewers trust it is real.
4. **The last 20 seconds.** The reason to act now, and where to start.

Polish is not what makes these films work. Jev's film looked like a documentary, and many launch films that do well are a founder and a screen recording. What they share is a person explaining why they built it, and proof you can see.

**Key pointers:**

- Aim for 1 to 3 minutes. Avoid the 30 to 60 second middle.
- Put the founder on camera and post from the founder's account.
- Measure the hero by signups and bookmarks; long films get fewer likes per view.

## Video 2: the thread clips (3 to 5 clips, one workflow each)

The hero film tells the story. The clips show the product doing specific jobs, so each viewer finds the one that matches theirs. Make 3 to 5 clips of 20 to 45 seconds, and give each one a single workflow and a one-line caption that says what it does.

Short clips earn more engagement per view. In the same set, videos under a minute got **3.5 to 3.7 likes per 1,000 views**, about twice the rate of 2 to 3 minute films. That makes them good replies, good quote posts and good material for creators.

InsForge's Launch Week used this format: after the hero film, a new feature clip went out each day, covering deployment, realtime, a remote MCP server, and the CLI with agent skills. Each clip had a clear job. The lesson from their numbers is about distribution, not the clips: posted from the company account alone, they drew about 1,000 to 9,600 views each.

![InsForge Launch Week announcement on X: InsForge 2.0, the backend for agentic development, 1.5M views](/blog/uploads/product-launch-video/insforge-launch-week-hero.webp "InsForge's Launch Week hero post, 1.5M views. Its daily feature clips followed from the company account.")

Post them like this:

- **In the first hour:** two clips as replies under the hero post, so people who open the thread see the product working.
- **Days 2 to 5:** one clip a day as its own post, each quoted by the founder and two or three people from the creator list.
- **Each clip gets a different poster:** an engineer who built the feature, a design partner, a creator whose audience needs that workflow.

For the script and recording of each clip, use the five beats in our [demo video script guide](/blog/how-to-script-a-technical-demo-video).

**Key pointers:**

- One workflow per clip, 20 to 45 seconds, captioned.
- Two clips in the thread on launch day, then one a day.
- Hand each clip to someone with their own audience. The company account alone is not enough.

## Video 3: the short cut (15 to 30 seconds, for ads and LinkedIn)

The short cut is for people who saw the launch, or should have: retargeting ads, LinkedIn, and anywhere a viewer gives you a few seconds. LinkedIn recommends video ads of [15 to 30 seconds](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a424737), so they qualify for all of its feed and Audience Network placements, and suggests adding subtitles.

Cut it from the hero film, but change the order:

- **Frame one is the product,** not the founder and not a logo.
- **One line of text on screen** says the claim from the brief.
- **Captions on everything,** so it works with the sound off.
- **End on the offer,** with the date it ends.

Launches with videos under 30 seconds still reached 1.22x a typical launch for small accounts in our data, with 3.5 likes per 1,000 views. The short cut is not a smaller hero film. It is the proof shot, the claim and the offer, with nothing in between.

**Key pointers:**

- 15 to 30 seconds, the product in the first frame, captions on.
- Run it to people who watched the hero film or visited your site during launch week.
- Use it on LinkedIn, where B2B buyers see it next to their work.

## Give people a reason to act this week

A launch should create a spike, and people act on a spike when something ends. Yet only **27 of 2,701 launch posts (1%)** in our data put an offer or a deadline in the post. Those reached **1.32x** a typical launch for the account size, against 1.17x for the rest. It is a small sample, but the bigger point is how rare it is: an offer makes your launch stand out from the other 99%.

Real launch offers from this year:

- **A free window.** [TinyFish Monitor](https://x.com/Tiny_Fish/status/2102430248092700765) launched as "Free for the next 72 hours!" and reached 499K views from an account with about 12,500 followers. [ElevenCreative](https://x.com/ElevenCreative/status/2105674217484374286) made Eleven v4 free for 11 days to celebrate the launch.
- **A discount with an end date.** [Cursor for iOS](https://x.com/cursor_ai/status/2071641103191998810) launched with Composer 2.5 at 75% off in the app "now through July 5", and passed 6.5M views.
- **A contest.** During its Launch Week, InsForge ran a 10-day contributor hackathon: $1,000 for first place, and $25 plus three months of free credits for everyone who merged a pull request.

![TinyFish Monitor launch post on X: free for the next 72 hours, 499.2K views](/blog/uploads/product-launch-video/tinyfish-launch-offer.webp "A launch with a deadline in the first lines: free for the next 72 hours.")

![InsForge PR Hackathon post from Launch Week: $1,000 first prize and $25 plus credits for every merged pull request](/blog/uploads/product-launch-video/insforge-launch-week-contest.webp "InsForge's Launch Week contest: a 10-day window and a reward for every contributor.")

For an AI or developer product, offers that work are free credits for the first signups, a free window on a paid tier, a founder onboarding call for teams that sign up this week, or a contest that ends on a date. Put the offer in the post's first lines and in the last 20 seconds of the hero film.

**Key pointers:**

- Give the launch an end date: credits, a free window, a discount or a contest.
- Put the offer in the first lines of the post, not only in the video.
- Pick an offer that leads to product use, like credits, not one that leads only to likes.

## The production plan: one shoot day, most of the effort on distribution

You don't need a studio for any of this. The films that travel are clear more than they are expensive, and the work that decides reach happens after the edit: who posts, who quotes, and when. Spend most of your time and budget there. Our [viral launch guide](/blog/viral-launch-on-x) covers creator budgets and the run sheet in detail.

Plan one shoot day and three edits:

```text
SHOOT DAY
- Founder on camera: the problem and why you built it (10 to 15 minutes of answers)
- Screen recordings: each workflow from the brief, recorded separately, with real data
- One uncut run of the main workflow, start to finish
- 5 to 10 seconds of the proof shot, recorded cleanly on its own

EDITS
- Hero film: 1 to 3 minutes, angle and proof first, offer last
- Clips: 3 to 5 cuts of 20 to 45 seconds, one workflow each, captioned
- Short cut: 15 to 30 seconds, product first, claim on screen, offer at the end

TIMELINE
- Day -14: brief written and approved
- Day -7:  shoot day
- Day -3:  all three edits done, posts written, offer live
- Day -1:  clips and launch time sent to everyone who will post or quote
- Day 0:   hero film from the founder; two clips in the thread within the hour
- Days 1-5: one clip a day, each with its own posters
- Week 2:  short cut runs as ads to everyone who watched or visited
```

**Key pointers:**

- One shoot day can produce all three videos if the brief comes first.
- Record the workflows separately, so each clip is a clean cut.
- Send clips to the people who will post them a day before launch, not on the day.

## FAQ

### How long should a product launch video be?

For the main launch film, 1 to 3 minutes. In 1,889 X launches with video from accounts under 100K followers, films of 2 to 3 minutes reached 2.83x a typical launch for the account size, and 30 to 60 second videos reached 0.78x. Make shorter cuts too: 20 to 45 second clips for the thread and 15 to 30 seconds for ads.

### How many videos do you need for a product launch?

Three kinds: one hero film, 3 to 5 short clips that each show one workflow, and one 15 to 30 second cut for ads and LinkedIn. The hero film starts the launch, the clips keep it in the feed for the rest of the week, and the short cut follows up with people who saw it.

### Should the founder be in the launch video?

Yes, when you can. People share people more readily than brand announcements, and the biggest breakout in our data, Jev, was a film of the founder explaining why he built it. Post the hero film from the founder's account and have the company account quote it.

### What is the difference between a launch video and a demo video?

A demo video shows how to do one job with the product. A launch video tells people why it matters now: the problem, the proof and a reason to act this week. The launch suite includes demo-style clips, but the hero film leads with the story and the claim.

### Do you need a production studio for a launch video?

No. A clear brief, a founder on camera, clean screen recordings and good captions matter more than a studio. Spend more on getting the videos seen, through the founder's network and creators, than on polishing the edit.
