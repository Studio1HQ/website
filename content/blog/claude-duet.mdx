## TL;DR

If you want the quick take, Claude Duet is Anthropic's answer to OpenAI's dots and Meta's Muse, and the first outside reviews are warm but not blind. It suits Claude subscribers who do knowledge work. It does not suit anyone who needs it this week from outside the US.

- Access is the catch. It is waitlist-only, for Pro ($20 a month) and Max (from $100) subscribers with a US Claude account. Team and Enterprise plans are not included.
- It costs no extra money, but it does cost limits. Its background checks, which run every few hours, draw from the same five-hour and weekly limits as normal Claude chats.
- Early verdict: Dan Shipper of Every spent about two weeks with it and called it the first personal agent he would call a pro tool. He also found weaker email drafts than ChatGPT's dot and scheduled runs that cannot reach custom connectors.
- The biggest gap against rivals: you cannot watch or take over its browser yet, and it only checks your accounts every few hours. Do not hand it anything truly urgent.

Meta, OpenAI, and now Anthropic have each shipped an always-on agent within about two months. The useful question isn't which one has the best demo. It's which one is still useful in week three, after it has misread a thread or eaten into your limits. Anthropic says both can happen.

This post pulls together Anthropic's own pages, the first independent reviews, and price and access comparisons, so you can decide before you spend time on a waitlist.

## What is Claude Duet?

Claude Duet is a separate Anthropic app where Claude works as a proactive agent. Anthropic opened a research preview on October 21, 2026. The company says it built Duet for professionals and small business owners who have more on their plate than they can track.

Here is what is actually in it, per Anthropic's announcement and its help center guide:

- **Model.** It runs on Claude Opus 5.5. You cannot pick another model, and Duet decides how much effort a job needs.
- **A morning brief.** Each morning it looks across your email and calendar, picks what needs you most, and can start the first steps, such as drafting replies.
- **Check-ins every few hours.** It watches your connected accounts and messages you only when something needs a decision. Anthropic says checking nonstop would use up plan limits much faster.
- **Its own cloud computer.** It has a virtual machine with its own browser, so it keeps working after you close the app.
- **Your Claude setup carries over.** Memory, skills, and connected apps come from your Claude account. You talk to it by typing or by voice, in one main thread, with separate channels for different topics.
- **Two tabs for work.** Tasks show what it is doing and what needs your input. Make it produce bigger outputs, such as a research brief, a financial model, a deck, or a dashboard.
- **Connections.** It works with more than 6,000 connectors and plugins from the Claude Marketplace, and it can use its own browser for sites that have no connector.

It lives in its own iOS and Android apps, and on claude.ai in a browser. For now, it is separate from the main Claude app.

Anthropic's product page also shows a sample morning brief for a fictional café owner, pulling in a point-of-sale system, Shopify, a CRM, a support inbox, payroll, and a bank. That is a demo, not a customer result. The real-world stories come from the reviews below.

## How does Claude Duet compare with OpenAI's dots and Meta's Muse?

All three give you an agent that has its own cloud computer, keeps working after you log off, and asks before it does anything consequential. Engadget and Tom's Guide covered dots and Muse before Duet arrived. Here is how the three line up on the facts those outlets and Anthropic have published.

|  | Claude Duet | OpenAI dots | Meta Muse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Model** | Claude Opus 5.5 | GPT-6 Astra | Muse Spark |
| **Where it lives** | Own iOS and Android apps, plus claude.ai | Inside ChatGPT | Own apps, the web, or WhatsApp |
| **Cheapest way in** | Claude Pro at $20 a month | ChatGPT Pro 100 at $100 a month | Free tier with a usage cap |
| **Higher tiers** | Max from $100 a month | Business Premium at $125 per user monthly ($100 billed annually) | $20 Power, $100 Maximum |
| **Availability** | US only, waitlist, Pro and Max | Eligible Pro and Business Premium users, outside the EEA, Switzerland, and UK | US and Canada |
| **Connections** | 6,000+ connectors and plugins | 4,000+ apps through ChatGPT plugins | Slack, Stripe, Shopify, QuickBooks, plus Facebook and Instagram business accounts |
| **Approval controls** | Asks before sending, paying, signing, or deleting | Custom rules and auto-review | Asks before sensitive actions, with read and write access kept separate |
| **Usage limits** | Shares your Claude plan limits, background work included | Does not count toward limits unless used inside ChatGPT Work or Codex | Capped on the free tier |

Three differences stand out.

**Price of entry.** Duet is the cheapest paid way into this category if you already pay for Claude. Muse is the only one with a free tier. Dots start at $100 a month for an individual, which Tom's Guide reads as a pitch to professionals and developer teams.

**The usage trade.** Duet's background work eats into the limits you also use for chat. Per Engadget, dots mostly do not. That is a real point for OpenAI.

**The browser.** Flavio Copes, who read through the documentation, says that dots and xAI's Grok Bot let you watch and take over their browsers, while Duet's preview does not. He calls that Duet's main weakness.

## What do early users say, and what have they built with Claude Duet?

It's only a few days old, so there isn't much yet. Here is what the people who actually used it say.

**Dan Shipper at Every** used it for about two weeks and calls it the first personal agent he would call a pro tool. He likes the channels, and that it keeps his docs, slides, and dashboards up to date. He doesn't like its email drafts, which he finds weaker than ChatGPT's dot, and scheduled runs can't use his custom connectors. Read his review.

**Peter Yang** built a morning brief, email triage, and a sponsor dashboard in his video, and calls Duet the most proactive agent he has tried. It hit a wall on things like Uber and the cloud browser. The video is sponsored by Wispr Flow.

**Claire Vo** tried it on inbox triage, her sales pipeline, and a sales deck. She loved the design, but it never nudged her and felt slow, so she still forgot to use it. Her post is worth a read, mostly because she and Yang disagree on whether it's proactive enough.

**Phil Simon** found that Notion worked with almost no setup and thinks Duet could replace Google Alerts.

Anthropic's own stories are company-picked, so take them as the best case. Amin Ebrahimi, who runs Shahs of Kabob with about 110 employees, used it to work through invoices store by store. Mya Dalton had it prep a client brief, and it offered to move a call that sat right before her flight.

### Worth trying first

- A morning brief and email triage, which is where Yang started.
- Unpaid invoice reminders. Anthropic's example: "Check Stripe for unpaid invoices and draft reminder emails for me to approve." Nothing goes out without your yes.
- "Pull together a brief on this company before my 2 pm meeting." Anthropic says Duet tells you which email or file each fact came from, so you can check it. Prompts from the help center.

## How much does Claude Duet cost?

| Plan | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Claude Pro | $20 a month, or $17 a month on annual billing ($200 up front) |
| Claude Max | From $100 a month, billed monthly |
| Claude Duet | No extra charge during the preview |

Plan prices come from Claude's pricing page.

### What it costs in limits

This is where the bill hides. Duet uses your plan's five-hour and weekly limits, at the same rate as chats, and its background work counts. That covers the morning brief and the check-ins. Bigger jobs like a financial model or a deck take more model turns and more background work. Anthropic's own wording is that you may hit your limits sooner than you would with chat alone.

The good news is a sweetener. When you are admitted, you get one full limit reset that clears both your five-hour and weekly limits, available until November 30, 2026.

A practical test, borrowed from Copes: after a normal week, check how much allowance is left for the rest of your Claude work. If Duet is taking too much, turn down what it watches.

### What it costs per purchase

Duet can buy things for your business using Link, Stripe's digital wallet, or Shopify's Shop Pay. Per the help center, each purchase uses a one-time card that only works for that purchase, and Duet asks you first. Copes adds from the support docs that Link needs a US payment method, does not support recurring payments, and that a merchant can still block checkout after you approve.

## How do you get access to Claude Duet?

You need a Pro or Max plan, a Claude account in the US, and an invitation from the waitlist. Invitations go out in waves.

1. Go to the Claude Duet product page and select Join the waitlist.
2. Enter your name and the email address on your Claude account.
3. Pick your plan and phone type, iPhone or Android. You can also say what you would hand off first.
4. Submit, then wait for the invitation email.
5. Open that email on your phone and follow the steps, using the Apple or Google account tied to the same email.
6. Sign in with your Claude account. It can take a few minutes for the app to appear.

Match your emails exactly. Anthropic says access is tied to the address you gave on the waitlist form.

## Is Claude Duet safe to connect to your email and money?

Anthropic puts safety near the top of its pitch, and the design choices are sensible. They are also still its own claims. The company says more detail is coming "in the coming days," so there is no full write-up to check yet.

What Anthropic says it has done:

- **Approvals.** Before Duet sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document, or deletes anything, it shows you the action and waits. If you are not around, it finishes the rest and brings the approval back later.
- **Hard stops.** Trading securities, moving money between accounts, and accessing someone else's account are off-limits even with your yes.
- **Passwords.** Duet never directly sees your passwords or sign-in tokens. When a site asks for one, you type it into a separate form on your own device.
- **A data classifier.** A new check reviews each message and is built to refuse sharing the strictest tier of data, such as credit card numbers and government ID numbers. Health and personal finance details go out only if you explicitly say so.
- **Outside testing.** Red teams and an external firm ran penetration tests, and Duet is covered by Anthropic's public bug bounty program.

What Anthropic admits it cannot promise: Duet reads email and web pages written by other people, and those can hide instructions meant to fool it. This is called prompt injection. Anthropic says no defense is perfect, which is why consequential actions need your approval.

Two control details are worth knowing. Disconnecting an app stops Duet from reaching it, but Duet keeps memory of what it already learned. And Duet can search your past Claude chats, which you can turn off under Settings, then Memory, using the Search and reference chats toggle.

## What should you check before you join?

- **Your location and plan.** US account, Pro or Max. Team and Enterprise are out for now.
- **Your email match.** Use the same address on the waitlist as on your Claude account, Apple ID, or Google account.
- **Your connectors.** Microsoft 365 is not available yet. Slack works with limits. Custom connectors may fail inside scheduled tasks.
- **Your limit headroom.** If you already hit your Claude limits most weeks, background work will make it worse.
- **How urgent your alerts are.** A check every few hours is fine for a brief. It is not fine for a payment deadline.
- **Whether you need to watch the browser; i**f you do, Duet's preview will frustrate you.
- **Your first test job.** Start with something low-risk, like invoice reminders you approve, and measure how often it gets them right.

## Where does Claude Duet still fall short?

Anthropic is upfront about several of these. The rest come from reviewers.

- **It can misjudge urgency.** Anthropic says it may misread a thread, and something can arrive between checks.
- **No browser view.** You cannot watch Duet in its browser or take it over.
- **Checks only every few hours.** By design, to protect your limits.
- **Weaker email drafts.** Shipper preferred the dots.
- **Custom connectors and scheduled runs do not mix**, per Every.
- **It cannot direct your Claude sessions.** It can see sessions on your computers, but not run them.
- **Voice is transcription only**, per Every.
- **Limited reach.** It cannot see files on your computer, cannot change your work in Claude, and does not make calls or send texts.
- **US only, and its own app.** It is not in the main Claude app yet.

## Which one should you pick: Claude Duet, dots, or Muse?

There is no single winner, and nobody has published a fair side-by-side test yet. Here is how the facts point.

- **Pick Claude Duet if** you already pay for Claude, have a US account, do knowledge work, and want Slack-style channels and finished docs, decks, and dashboards back. Shipper's pro-tool verdict is aimed at you.
- **Pick OpenAI's dots if** you are on ChatGPT Pro or a business seat, want to watch and take over the browser, or want an agent whose usage does not mostly count against your limits.
- **Pick Meta's Muse if** you want to try an agent without paying for another subscription. Tom's Guide leans this way, with a caution to read its permissions carefully.
- **Wait if** you are outside the US, on Team or Enterprise, or mostly want help with personal errands. Those are the gaps right now.
