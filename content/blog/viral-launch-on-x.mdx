**The short answer:** A launch goes viral on X when you engineer the first hour. Size the launch from a revenue goal, lead with a one-sentence angle instead of your product name, and post a native film of 1 to 3 minutes from the founder's account. Line up 30 to 60 creators who quote it in their own words within the hour, answer replies for two hours, post a second beat, and launch again next week.

This guide is built from four sources:

- **Our own data.** We analyzed 2,575 product launch posts on X from January 7 to November 13, 2026, with views, likes, bookmarks, video length and follower counts read from X.
- **The agencies.** We read the public playbooks, case studies and price lists of 11 agencies and studios that run X launches for startups and big tech companies.
- **X's code.** We checked X's open-source ranking code as it stood on November 14, 2026.
- **15 launches.** We followed launches that broke out between 2025 and 2026, including several that went wrong.

If you are launching a developer tool, read this with our [guide to launching a developer tool on X](/blog/how-to-launch-a-developer-tool-on-x). It covers installs, open source and devtool creators.

## What 2,575 launches on X in 2026 tell you about going viral

The posts come from a public directory of X launches that records each launch post with its views, likes, bookmarks, video length and the posting account's follower count. Together they drew 5.4 billion views. The median launch got 569K views, and 36% passed 1 million.

To compare a 2,000-follower founder with a 2-million-follower lab fairly, we fitted views against followers and measured each launch against what a typical launch from an account that size gets. "1.25x" below means 25% more views than that.

![Chart of 2,575 X launches in 2026: top 10% took 67% of views, and 24% of launches from 1K to 10K follower accounts passed 1M views](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/launch-data.webp "2,575 product launches on X, Jan 7 to Nov 13, 2026. Analyzed by Studio1.")

1. **Virality is a power law.** The top 10% of launches took 67% of all views, and the top 1% took 26%. One launch that breaks out is worth more than ten that do fine.
2. **Followers matter less than you think.** Across the set, 10x more followers came with only about 2x more views. The median launch from an account with 1K to 10K followers reached 85x its follower count, and 24% of them passed 1M views.
3. **The first line moves reach.** First lines that named the product reached 0.85x a typical launch for the account size. First lines that led with a claim and no name reached 1.13x.
4. **Comparisons travel.** Posts that compared ("vs", "faster than", "better than") reached 1.64x and got the most bookmarks per view.
5. **Longer films helped smaller accounts.** Under 100K followers, videos of 60 to 180 seconds reached 1.42x typical, against 0.80x for videos under a minute.
6. **Weekends lose.** Saturday launches reached 0.49x and Sunday 0.61x. Wednesday led at 1.15x. Only 4% of launches went out on a weekend.
7. **Some reach is bought.** Of the 927 launches past 1M views, 125 (13%) had fewer than 0.3 likes per 1,000 views. The median across all launches was 2.8.
8. **The first launch is rarely the biggest.** 338 companies launched more than once. For 46% of them the biggest launch came later, and for companies with 3 or more launches it was 57%.

Read it as a study of launches that got noticed. A directory like this lists fewer of the launches that flopped, so treat the numbers as patterns, not odds.

**Key pointers:**

- Plan for a power law: one launch that breaks out beats several average ones.
- A small account can still break out. Audience fit and the first hour matter more than follower count.
- Judge reach next to likes per 1,000 views, or you will mistake paid reach for interest.

## How launch agencies think about a viral launch

The agencies that launch AI startups and big tech products on X publish surprisingly detailed playbooks. They disagree on tactics, but they share a philosophy.

1. **Virality is a system.** One agency's playbook puts it plainly: "Virality is a system, not a campaign." The same playbook explains why the posts must land close together: posts spread across a week produce "a slope nothing notices," while concentrated posts produce a spike the ranking system is built to detect.
2. **Start from revenue, not views.** "Views are the easy thing to get and the wrong thing to want," says another, which launches clients once or twice a week. It sizes each launch from a revenue goal, and its rule of thumb is new MRR in the first month about equal to what the launch cost.
3. **People repost people.** In most playbooks the hero post comes from the founder's account, and the company account quotes it.
4. **The angle is the launch.** The first line is "the angle, alone": no greeting, no "excited to announce", no product name.
5. **Fit beats follower count.** Every agency we read picks creators by audience fit over follower count. One scores them on fit first, recent engagement second and size last.
6. **The video is a third of it.** "A finished video is maybe 30% of a launch," says one studio that makes launch films. Everything around the video does the rest.
7. **Everything is a launch.** Features, benchmarks, integrations and a take on this week's model release all get the launch treatment, on a fixed slot, twice a week.

They split on the details. Here is where they disagree, and what our data and X's code say:

| Question | What agencies do | What the data and code say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Link in the post or the first reply? | Three put it in the post; one estimates a reply link "converts about ten times worse." Two put it in the reply. | X's weights have no link penalty, and X's then head of product said in July 2026 that links no longer need to go in replies. Put it in the post, above "Show more." |
| How long should the video be? | Most say 30 to 90 seconds. Two favor 60 to 120 seconds or longer. | In our set, 60 to 180 seconds won for accounts under 100K followers. |
| How tight is the creator window? | About 20 quotes in 10 minutes; a 4-hour window; or insiders first and creators at 30 and 60 minutes. | X's small-account lift lasts 2 hours. Concentrate inside it. |
| Send supporters the direct link? | Two never do. One sends it the second the post is live. | X's code ignores engagement from people who open a post directly. Let them find it in their feed. |
| Guarantee views? | Four sell view guarantees or impression floors, up to 5M views. Two call guarantees a red flag. | 13% of launches past 1M views look bought. A guarantee is easiest to hit with reach nobody cares about. |
| Clear or provocative? | Some push divisive hooks. Others say "clear, not clever." | Short, plain statements reached 1.25x. Posts claiming a "first" got 53% more views than the rest but 1.6 likes per 1,000 views against 3.0. |

**Key pointers:**

- Write the revenue goal down before anyone writes a post.
- Treat the first hour as the product of the launch, and the video as one input.
- When agencies disagree, check the data or X's code, not the loudest playbook.

## How X turns a launch post into a cascade

X publishes its ranking code as [xai-org/x-algorithm](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm). We rechecked it on November 14, 2026. Each post is scored on the predicted chance that a viewer replies, quotes, shares, follows and so on, times a weight per action. The [parameter file](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs) gives copy-link shares a weight of 20, replies, quotes and DM shares 5, following the author 4, reposts 1, likes 0.708 and link opens 0.2. Our [devtool launch guide](/blog/how-to-launch-a-developer-tool-on-x#how-x-decides-who-sees-your-launch-post) has the full table.

![Four-stage launch cascade on X: seed two weeks out, spark in minutes 0 to 10, spread to hour 2, sustain to hour 48](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/launch-cascade.webp "How a launch post spreads on X. The first ten minutes are framed.")

Four rules in the code decide how a launch spreads:

1. **A quote is worth five reposts.** A quote also lands the post in the creator's followers' feeds with the creator's own take on top. That is why every agency's plan is built on quotes, not reposts.
2. **Small accounts get two hours.** The [cold-start scorer](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/main/home-mixer/scorers/author_cold_start.rs) lifts posts from accounts with 50,000 followers or fewer until the post is 7,200 seconds old or has 200 Home impressions.
3. **Posts older than 48 hours drop out.** `MAX_POST_AGE` is set to 48 hours in `home-mixer/params/config.rs`. Each launch, and each day of a launch week, starts a new clock.
4. **Group chats don't count.** A comment in the parameter file says engagement only counts on a post served in the Home timeline: "Directly navigating to a post (i.e., coordinating via groupchat) has no ranking impact." One agency's rule follows from it: creators get a ping with no link and find the pinned post on the profile, because "Thirty clicks on one pasted link look like a group chat."

**Key pointers:**

- Ask for quotes with a take, not reposts or likes.
- On an account under 50,000 followers, put the creator wave inside the first two hours.
- Never paste the launch link into a group chat and ask for likes. It does nothing for ranking.

## Step 1: Size the launch from a revenue goal (two weeks out)

Agencies plan with a simple funnel. One uses about 1,000 site visits per 100K views as its base case, with a real range of 300 to 6,000. Work back from the result you need:

| Line | Example | Your numbers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | 20 new paying customers | |
| Trial to paid | 8% (one B2B tool an agency launches converts 8 to 9% of trials) | |
| Trials needed | 250 | |
| Visit to trial | 5% (measure your own landing page first) | |
| Visits needed | 5,000 | |
| Visits per 100K views | 1,000 (base case) | |
| Views needed | 500K, about the median launch in our data | |

If the views you need are well above 1M, fix the landing page or the offer before you spend on reach. Then set up the tracking that lets you judge the launch later:

- **A link per creator.** Give every creator their own UTM link.
- **A promo code per launch.** One agency caps the code at 200 uses, then raises it to 500 as a second post on launch day.
- **A signup question.** Ask "Where did you hear about us?" at signup.
- **A fixed slot.** Book the date and minute, and keep the account quiet for six hours either side.

**Key pointers:**

- Write the target in signups, demos or MRR, not views.
- Decide what counts as a conversion before launch day, and tag every link.
- Book the next two launches now, so one bad day doesn't decide the channel.

## Step 2: Find the angle, then write the first line

The angle is one sentence that makes someone stop scrolling. One agency works from five recurring angles:

- **A news tie-in.** This week's big release, and your product doing something with it the same day.
- **An uncomfortable number.** A benchmark or stat your category would rather not see.
- **An impossible-looking demo.** One take, no cuts, a real task.
- **A useful artifact.** A template, dataset or stack people save.
- **A stand.** Something you believe that most of your category doesn't.

The launches in our data that beat their follower counts by the widest margin opened with lines like these. Views and follower counts are from the directory:

| Angle | First line | Views | Followers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A stand | ["AI is making kids dumber."](https://x.com/suekhim/status/2060378988606878147) | 4.9M | 15.9K |
| A result | ["We switched 10 people from iPhone to Blue. Weeks later, none of them want their iPhone back."](https://x.com/OmarAbcXyz123/status/2120579219868835915) | 9.6M | 6.9K |
| A problem everyone has | ["Everyone owns skincare products, but nobody knows what's actually working."](https://x.com/malhalla06/status/2088003399690420314) | 3.3M | 3.1K |
| A comparison | ["we just built git for video editing."](https://x.com/lucashjin/status/2036805674491875424) | 2.0M | 2.5K |
| An anti-position | ["I made the anti-Grammarly. Mess up your emails with AI."](https://x.com/horwitzben/status/2047293550342152680) | 2.2M | 6.2K |
| A raise as a promise | ["We've raised $1.3M from YC & Afore Capital so you never have to type or speak again."](https://x.com/AarushYadav47/status/2116198463847408066) | 12.2M | 1.9K |

None of them starts with the product name. The data backs that up:

![Bar chart of first lines vs reach: Excited to 0.54x, Today we 0.74x, names product 0.85x, short statement 1.25x, comparison 1.64x](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/hooks.webp "How the opening line changed reach, adjusted for follower count. 2,403 launches.")

- **"Excited to announce" and "Thrilled to share"** reached 0.54x. The sample is small (31 posts), but no other opener did worse.
- **"Today we..."** reached 0.74x and **"Introducing..."** 1.01x.
- **First lines of 9 to 14 words** did best (1.16x). Lines of 25 words or more reached 0.71x.

Then build the rest of the post the way Blue did. Its first line is a result. The product comes second, then what it does, who it's for, the ask and the proof. The link sits above "Show more", and the film plays natively.

![Blue's X launch post: a result as the first line, the product second, who it's for, a CTA, investor proof, link, and a 3-minute film](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/blue-launch-post.webp "Blue's launch: 9.6M views from an account with about 7K followers. Screenshot taken on X, November 14, 2026.")

```text
[The angle, alone: a result, a stand, a number or a comparison. Under 15 words.]

Introducing [product]: [what it is, in plain words].

[What it does that people care about, in one or two lines.]

[Who it's for: "We built it for people who..."]

[The ask: try it, get yours, join today.]
[Proof: backers, users, a benchmark.]

[link, above "Show more"]
[native video, burned-in subtitles, the interesting frame first]
```

**Key pointers:**

- Write 15 to 20 first lines and test them on people outside the team. Keep the one they repeat back to you.
- Lead with the claim. Put the product name in the second line.
- Keep the first line under 15 words, and never open with "excited to announce."

## Step 3: Make the launch film

92% of the launches in our data had a native video, and the median ran 66 seconds. Length mattered most for accounts with fewer than 100K followers:

![Bar chart: for accounts with 500 to 10K followers, 60 to 180 second videos reached 1.35x typical vs 0.64x for under 60 seconds](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/film-length.webp "Launch video length vs reach, by account size. 2,575 launches on X in 2026.")

There is a catch. The longer films got 2.1 likes per 1,000 views against 3.5 for short ones, which suggests many of them came with paid or creator distribution. A long film earns its length by telling a story. Brilliant's Koji launch opens with a stand, then a film of a little over two minutes:

![Koji's X launch post: "AI is making kids dumber. It should be making them geniuses." with a two-minute film and 4.9M views](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/koji-launch-post.webp "Koji's launch: 4.9M views and 10K bookmarks. Screenshot taken on X, November 14, 2026.")

Blue's film, above, runs 3 minutes 19 seconds.

What the agencies and studios agree on:

- **The interesting frame comes first.** Show the product or the result inside 3 to 5 seconds. No logo intro.
- **Subtitles are burned in.** Most people watch launch videos muted.
- **Upload the video natively**, landscape for software, never as a YouTube link.
- **Lo-fi works when the product is the show.** Fundamental's founder called his Shortcut video ["two minutes, no frills,"](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video) with him on screen for about 15 seconds. It drew 4.1M impressions on X.

A beat sheet for a 90-second film:

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 5s | The problem or the result, on screen, with a subtitle |
| 5 to 30s | The product doing the thing, in one take |
| 30 to 75s | Proof: a real task, a number, a user, a comparison |
| 75 to 90s | Who it's for and what to do next |

Launch video studios charge from about $10,000 for an animated video to $20,000 for a launch film, with 4 to 6 weeks of production. If you need one made, our [developer video production team](/developer-video-production) makes launch films and demos, and our guide covers [how to script a technical demo video](/blog/how-to-script-a-technical-demo-video).

**Key pointers:**

- If your account has under 100K followers, plan a film of 60 to 120 seconds with a story, not a feature tour.
- The first frame has to work as a still image, with sound off.
- Start the video work at least three weeks out, working back from the launch story, as Fundamental's founder advises.

## Step 4: Build the creator wave

The creator wave is what agencies are really selling. Here is how they run it.

**How many.** At least 30 creators, 40 to 60 if the budget allows. Bigger launches go further: one agency seeded a launch to 80 to 100 accounts, and a studio ran about 300 accounts in three layers:

- About 30 top voices, each writing their own quote post
- About 90 in a "reach layer"
- About 180 in a "momentum layer" for likes and reposts

Paying accounts only to like and repost is the part X's rules are written against, so keep paid work to quote posts with real opinions.

**Who goes first.** That studio puts the people who know the founder first: investors quote at 5 minutes, friends comment at 15, and creators come in at 30 and 60 minutes. "The first hour isn't the influencer layer at all," it writes.

**How to pick.** Check each creator's last ten posts:

- Do the replies come from real people in your audience?
- Are the views in line with the follower count?
- Was the last paid post more than a week ago?

**How to brief.** Give each creator one of two or three angles, product access a week early and a folder of clips. Ask for their own words. One agency explains why: "Thirty people posting the same sentence reads as a script… Thirty people each saying what struck them reads as news." Another pastes screenshots of strong quote posts from past launches into the brief, so creators see what good looks like. Ask every creator to follow the launching account the day before.

**What it costs.** One agency's published rates:

| Creator | Price per quote post |
| --- | --- |
| Builders with 2K to 5K followers | About $50 |
| The median creator | About $100 (from $20) |
| Larger accounts, 2 or 3 per launch | $250 to $400 |
| Fans of the product | Often free |

That agency won't run a launch under $4,000. It also warns about a $500 launch that reached 13K people and brought one signup.

**How to pay.** Agree on the price, the posting window and what counts as delivered, in writing. Pay only when the quote is live in the window and still up a day later: "No post, no payment."

**How to stay inside X's rules.** X's [Paid Partnerships policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/paid-partnerships-policy) requires the "Paid Partnership" label on paid posts, including gifted access. Its [authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity) bans coordinated inauthentic activity and engagement swaps. A brief with angles and real opinions stays on the right side. Identical copy, bought likes and pods don't.

Our [developer influencer marketing guide](/blog/developer-influencer-marketing) covers contracts and disclosure in depth, and our [strategy post](/blog/developer-influencer-marketing-strategy) covers how to score creators for fit.

**Key pointers:**

- Get 30 or more written yeses, not 30 messages sent.
- Give each creator an angle and access, never a script.
- Pay after the post has been live for 24 hours, and label every paid post.

## Step 5: Run launch day by the clock

![Seven-step launch run sheet from T−14 days to T+3 days, with the first 10 minutes after posting framed](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/run-sheet.webp "A viral launch on X, from two weeks out to day three.")

**Twelve hours before, run a go/no-go check.** One agency won't launch until all eight of these are true:

- [ ] The creator budget is set aside
- [ ] The final post is approved and frozen
- [ ] 30 or more creators have confirmed in writing
- [ ] Every creator has an angle, a draft and the assets
- [ ] Your network is told and the team is briefed
- [ ] The link shows above "Show more" and opens
- [ ] The reply plan is written and the first replies are drafted
- [ ] Target numbers are written down

**Give everyone one job.** The owner runs the launch. The founder publishes and replies. One person counts quote posts and messages missing creators. Teammates write their own quotes and cover replies. Someone else handles the creators and payments. The agency that wrote this calls doing publishing, counting and replying as one job a mistake.

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| T−3h | Confirm every creator. Replace anyone who hasn't said yes. |
| T−1h | Founder online, creators in the chat, team at desks. |
| T0 | Founder publishes by hand, pins the post and adds the first reply with the link. A second person checks it logged out. |
| Right after | Creators get the "it's live" ping with no link. The launch email goes out. |
| T+10m | About 20 quote posts are live. Message anyone missing by name. |
| T+15m to T+2h | Founder answers every reply. Around 35 minutes in, invite questions and answer them for an hour. |
| T+1h | Post a second beat: a number so far, a backstage clip or the best reply. |
| T+2h | Move warm engagers to DMs, one at a time. No automated DMs. |

**Key pointers:**

- If a gate fails, move the slot. Launching with open risks is "a NO-GO with better manners."
- Have someone check the live post logged out before creators get the ping.
- The founder stays in the replies, including the hard ones, for at least two hours.

## Step 6: Keep it going for 48 hours, then launch again

X stops recommending a post after 48 hours, so the strongest launches post a second beat inside that window:

- **Results.** When ChatGPT's image generation took off in March 2025, Sam Altman posted that ChatGPT had [added one million users in an hour](https://www.engadget.com/ai/chatgpt-gained-one-million-new-users-in-an-hour-today-201314746.html). OpenAI's Bill Peebles posted that [Sora hit 1M downloads](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/09/sora-hit-1m-downloads-faster-than-chatgpt/) faster than ChatGPT did. Each results post started its own news cycle.
- **"Available now."** Fundamental's follow-up video for Shortcut added [another 3.5M impressions](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video) on top of the 4.1M of the reveal.
- **A raised cap.** One agency launches with a promo code capped at 200 uses and raises it to 500 later the same day.

Then close the loop over three days:

- **Day 1:** check every creator post is still up and correct, pay, and thank them.
- **Day 2:** turn the people who engaged into a scored lead list.
- **Day 3:** compare results with the targets you wrote down, and book the next slot.

The repeat-launch data is the reason to keep going. For 46% of companies that launched more than once, the biggest launch wasn't the first. One agency's client ran 12 launches in five weeks for 1.99M total views.

**Key pointers:**

- Draft the second beat before launch day, and post it inside 48 hours.
- Treat every feature, benchmark and integration as the next launch.
- Keep two fixed slots a week with a day between them.

## 8 moves that make a launch travel, beyond the agency playbook

Agencies sell the creator wave. The launches that went furthest in 2025 and 2026 added at least one of these.

1. **Let people discover it before they know it's yours.** Google ran its image model anonymously on LMArena, where users called it ["nano banana"](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/how-nano-banana-got-its-name/), and Googlers dropped banana hints on X. Within about a week the Gemini app had [10M+ new users and 200M+ image edits](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/04/gemini-app-nano-banana/).
2. **Make the output easy to copy.** Ghibli-style images and [Sora cameos](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/openais-new-social-app-is-filled-with-terrifying-sam-altman-deepfakes) spread because users posted their own, not because of OpenAI's launch post. Sam Altman made his own likeness available to everyone on Sora, so the founder became the meme.
3. **Stage the day across press, investors and users.** Town announced its $55M Series A with a [Fortune exclusive](https://fortune.com/2026/06/03/towns-ai-assistants-andreessen-horowitz-forerunner-55-million/), investor memos and beta users posting screenshots, all on the same day. The [founder's post got close to 1M views](https://onbackgroundintel.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-funding-announcement).
4. **Trade something real for replies.** Fundamental asked viewers to comment for an early invite code. Replies are worth 5 in X's weights, and the commenters become your first users.
5. **Pick a fight your audience already cares about.** Anthropic's Super Bowl ads ran with the line "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." They drew a [public reply from Sam Altman](https://fortune.com/2026/02/09/super-bowl-ads-anthropic-openai-rivalry-trash-talk-ai-agent-war/). Claude's daily downloads [rose 3.6x](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/claude-super-bowl-mobile-growth).
6. **Stage a stunt offline and post it.** Slate Auto parked a truck in Venice, CA with a [different fake business ad each day](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/06/04/slate-auto-ev-marketing-strategy) before its reveal. It passed [100,000 refundable reservations](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/12/slate-auto-crosses-100000-refundable-reservations-in-two-weeks) in two weeks.
7. **Use scarcity only if there's a date.** Sora was invite-only in two countries and still [beat ChatGPT's first-week iOS installs](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/09/sora-hit-1m-downloads-faster-than-chatgpt/), 627K to 606K. A waitlist with no opening date just loses people.
8. **Make the launch findable afterwards.** Viral reach fades in 48 hours. A launch page, a changelog and a post that explains the product are what search engines and AI assistants pick up later. Our [AEO guide](/blog/answer-engine-optimization-guide) shows how.

**Key pointers:**

- Pick one move that fits your product, in addition to the creator wave.
- Plan press, investors and users for the same morning as the X post.
- Publish a page that will still answer questions a month later.

## When viral goes wrong

Views are the easy part. These launches got them and still show what to avoid. Our companion post, [why viral launches don't convert](/blog/why-viral-launches-dont-convert), covers the funnel side in depth.

![Cluely's launch post on X: "Cluely is out. cheat on everything." with a 1:32 cinematic video and 13.3M views](/blog/uploads/viral-launch-on-x/cluely-launch-post.webp "Cluely's April 2025 launch post. Screenshot taken on X, November 14, 2026.")

- **Controversy without conversion.** Cluely's "cheat on everything" film has 13.3M views. Its founder later [wrote that one UGC video got 49 million views and fewer than 100 downloads](https://viral.app/blog/cluely-viral-strategy), and in 2026 he [admitted to overstating revenue](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/05/cluely-ceo-roy-lee-admits-to-publicly-lying-about-revenue-numbers-last-year/).
- **Buying the zeitgeist.** Friend spent $1 million on 11,000 New York subway ads. Its founder said "I purchased the zeitgeist." [Fortune counted](https://fortune.com/2025/10/01/who-is-avi-schiffmann-friend-ai-pendant-necklace/) about 3,000 units sold and roughly $348,000 in revenue.
- **Claims the replies take apart.** 1X launched its NEO home robot with a ten-minute film. The coverage that followed focused on how much of its work [relies on remote human operators](https://www.engadget.com/ai/1x-neo-is-a-20000-home-robot-that-will-learn-chores-via-teleoperation-040252200.html).
- **Bought reach.** In our data, 125 launches passed 1M views with fewer than 0.3 likes per 1,000 views. If an agency guarantees views, ask how it gets them.
- **One launch, then silence.** A single launch is one draw from a wide spread. Several launches show which angle sells.

**Key pointers:**

- Test the provocative line on five customers. If it makes them less likely to buy, cut it.
- Have someone outside the team try to break every claim before launch.
- Report earned and paid reach separately.

## What to measure after a viral launch

"Knowing a launch did 2M views tells you nothing actionable," as one launch agency puts it. Track these per launch and per creator:

| Metric | Benchmark | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quote posts at T+10m and T+60m | About 20 at 10 minutes, every creator by 60 | Tells you if the wave landed inside the cold-start window |
| Likes per 1,000 views | Median 2.8 in our data; under 0.3 suggests paid reach | Separates interest from bought reach |
| Bookmarks per 1,000 views | Median 1.2 in our data | People save what they plan to try |
| Site visits per 100K views | About 1,000 (range 300 to 6,000) | Shows how much reach turned into traffic |
| New followers | 300 to 800 per launch, per one agency | Builds the audience for the next launch |
| Signups, demos, MRR per launch | First-month new MRR about equal to the launch's cost | The number that decides the next launch |

If the views came but the signups didn't, work through the [five leaks that stop viral launches converting](/blog/why-viral-launches-dont-convert). Two weeks later, check what lasted: branded searches, mentions in communities and whether AI assistants name your product. Our guide to [measuring visibility in AI answers](/blog/how-to-measure-devtool-visibility-in-ai-answers) shows how.

**Key pointers:**

- Give every creator their own link and code before launch day.
- Rebook creators on conversions per dollar, not views.
- Run a retro within three days, while the replies are fresh.

## FAQ

### How do you make a launch go viral on X?

Engineer the first hour. Lead with a one-sentence angle, post a native film of 1 to 3 minutes from the founder's account, and line up 30 to 60 creators who quote it in their own words within the hour. Answer replies for two hours, post a second beat within 48 hours, and launch again the next week.

### How many creators do you need for a viral X launch?

Most agencies plan for 30 to 60 creators. Some launches go much bigger: one agency seeded a launch to 80 to 100 accounts, and a studio ran about 300 in three layers. Fit matters more than count. Creators whose followers are your buyers beat larger accounts with the wrong audience.

### How much does a viral launch on X cost?

Creator quotes run from about $20 to $400 each, with a median near $100. One agency won't run a launch on less than $4,000 of creator budget. A managed launch from that agency costs $990 a month plus $2,000 per 100K views, capped at $10,000 per launch. A launch film from a studio costs $10,000 to $20,000.

### What is the best day to launch on X?

Tuesday to Thursday. In our analysis of 2,575 launches, Wednesday reached 1.15x a typical launch for the account size, Tuesday 1.07x and Thursday 1.03x. Saturday reached 0.49x and Sunday 0.61x. Morning US time is the common choice, but the creator wave matters more than the hour.

### Should the link go in the launch post or in a reply?

In the post, above "Show more." X's published ranking weights have no link penalty, and X's head of product [said in July 2026](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/2082217171506344297) that links no longer need to go in replies. Write the post so it works without the link, then repeat the link in the first reply.

### How long should a launch video on X be?

For accounts with under 100K followers, 60 to 180 seconds. Those films reached 1.42x a typical launch in our data, against 0.80x for videos under a minute. Show the product or result in the first 3 to 5 seconds, burn in subtitles and upload natively.

### Is it allowed to pay creators to quote your launch on X?

Yes, if each paid post carries X's "Paid Partnership" label and the creator writes it in their own words. X's rules ban coordinated inauthentic activity, engagement swaps and bought engagement. Identical copy from 30 accounts looks like exactly that, and likes from a group chat don't count for ranking anyway.
