## TL;DR

* An illustrative US hiring scenario comes to about $195,000 annually, assuming a $136,300 base salary and benefits equal to 30% of total compensation. This is not a verified DevRel salary average.
* SaaSHero estimates DevRel agency retainers at $8,000–$28,000 per month. Actual quotes depend on scope, staffing, and contract terms.
* An in-house hire suits ongoing ownership and close product collaboration. An agency can provide specialist expertise and additional execution capacity.
* A hybrid setup combines an internal owner with external support for agreed projects or ongoing work.

[Stack Overflow's 2025 survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai) found that 84% of developers use or plan to use AI tools, up from 76% the year before. The same survey found that 46% of developers do not trust what those tools tell them, up from 31%. These findings show a gap between AI adoption and trust in its accuracy. They do not establish how often developers check original sources.

![banner-image](/blog/uploads/image-6-.png)

That changes what you need from developer relations. Someone has to make your docs, tutorials, and community answers good enough to be quoted, and good enough to survive a double-check. The question is who that someone should be: a person on your payroll, or an agency on a retainer.

This guide compares the cost and timeline of each option, and tells you when to pick which.

### The salary is not the real cost

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics says that in June 2026, [wages were 70% of what private employers spend on a worker, and benefits were the other 30%](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/compensation-costs-for-private-industry-workers-averaged-46-89-per-hour-worked-in-june-2026.htm). That puts benefits at roughly 43% on top of salary.

* **Mid-level advocate:** $136,300 in salary becomes about $195,000 a year.
* **Senior advocate:** $171,300 in salary becomes about $245,000 a year.

Then come the extras:

* **Recruiter fees.** [15 to 25% of first-year pay is common](https://dev.to/d_v_/time-to-hire-for-engineers-95-days-in-2026-2dbn) for agency placements. On $136,300, that is roughly $20,000 to $34,000.
* **A program budget.** Events, travel, swag, community tools, and hosting for demos.
* **Gaps.** One person has one set of strengths and one calendar. If they quit, the hiring clock starts over.

### What does the quote usually leave out?

Two things often sit outside the retainer. [MarketerHire's devtools guide](https://marketerhire.com/blog/devtools-marketing-agency) lists paid channel spend of $5,000 to $30,000 a month and conference sponsorships of $10,000 to $50,000 per event as separate costs. And most agencies start with a trial or a minimum term. [Draft.dev says most clients begin with a 3-month pilot](https://draft.dev/learn/top-technical-content-marketing-agencies), and [Infrasity asks for a 3- to 6-month minimum](https://infrasity.com/).

Ask what is excluded before you sign. Ask whether the person you meet on the sales call is the person who does the work.

### Where is the break-even?

One fully loaded advocate at $195,000 a year works out to about **$16,200 a month**. If an agency retainer stays under that, year one is cheaper than hiring. Above it, you are paying more than a hire for what is often a shorter, narrower commitment.

## How long until each option starts working?

Nobody publishes a clean time-to-hire number for DevRel roles, so we use engineering hiring data, which is the closest match. Sourced numbers are linked. The rest are our own estimates and are marked.

### In-house timeline

* **Write the role and get it approved:** 1 to 2 weeks.
* **Hire:** about 9 weeks. [Proxify reports 62 days](https://proxify.io/knowledge-base/recruitment-and-hiring/why-does-hiring-a-software-engineer-take-35-days) as the global average for engineering roles. [Recruiting from Scratch](https://www.recruitingfromscratch.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-hire-a-senior-software-engineer-in-2026) says 47 days from opening a role to an accepted offer for senior engineers.
* **Notice period:** [two weeks is the usual custom in the US](https://fabrichq.ai/blogs/notice-period). In India, [60 to 90 days is common at tech companies](https://www.zoho.com/in/payroll/academy/hr-and-payroll/notice-period.html).
* **Learning your product:** 2 to 4 weeks.
* **First public work:** depends on what you focus on. A docs fix or a tutorial can go live in the first few weeks, while community and event work takes longer.
* **Results you can measure:** often 3 to 6 months out. [Stackmatix says to expect roughly one to two quarters before meaningful activation results appear](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/developer-marketing-agency).

### Agency timeline

* **Kickoff and audit:** 2 to 4 weeks. Ask for dates in writing.
* **First content live:** within 1 to 2 weeks of kickoff. [Infrasity lists 7 days for a technical video](https://infrasity.com/).
* **Early signals:** around 90 days. [Stateshift says most programs show early impact within 90 days](https://blog.stateshift.com/devrel-roi-metrics-how-to-measure-communitys-business-value/) if you track the right things, such as how many people finish a quickstart.
* **Community trust:** longer. An agency can ship tutorials fast, but trust in a community still builds slowly.

## When does an in-house DevRel hire make sense?

Hire in-house when most of these are true:

* **Your product is complex and changes often.** Someone has to sit in engineering conversations and understand why things work the way they do.
* **You have a real community to look after.** A Discord, a GitHub project, or a forum needs someone who shows up every day.
* **You can wait 3 to 6 months for proof.** Set early milestones for onboarding and delivery, alongside longer-term goals for adoption and community development.
* **You can protect a budget line.** A person with no budget has little to work with and is easy to cut.
* **You want developer feedback to reach your product team.** An employee can participate directly in product planning. External practitioners need agreed access and a clear feedback process.

## When does a DevRel agency make sense?

An agency is the better choice when:

* **You need docs, tutorials, or comparison pages in weeks.** Waiting for a hire means waiting for the content too.
* **You are not sure DevRel will work for you.** A 3- to 6-month contract is a cheaper experiment than a $195,000 hire.
* **You have a short push.** A launch, a new SDK, a hackathon.
* **You cannot find or afford a senior person.** In the [Draft.dev 2026 developer marketing survey](https://draft.dev/2026-developer-marketing-survey), 42% of teams said they outsource content creation.
* **You want steady routine work done well.** Checking docs for gaps, refreshing old posts, tracking what AI tools say about you.

Agencies have weak spots too. They do not know your product the way an employee does, and some put junior staff on your account after a senior pitch. [MarketerHire argues](https://marketerhire.com/blog/developer-marketing-agency) that one senior fractional hire can beat an agency that spreads junior people across your work.

## Can you use both?

Yes, and this is where many teams land. One person inside the company owns strategy, community, and product feedback. An agency handles production: tutorials, docs fixes, sample apps, and comparison pages.

Agencies sell this setup too. Hackmamba, for example, offers [a marketing leader from $10,000 a month](https://hackmamba.io/pricing) to set strategy, priorities, and reporting for your existing team. We agree with the idea. The person who knows your product decides what to make. The agency makes more of it, faster. ## How does AI search change the decision?

A developer who used to search Google for "best error tracking tool for Rails" now often asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and reads one answer. [HackerNoon's dev tool checklist](https://hackernoon.com/the-dev-tool-aeo-checklist) describes the moment when that answer names your competitor and not you. Getting named inside the answer is called answer engine optimization, or AEO.

![image.png](/blog/uploads/image-7-.png)

A few facts worth knowing:

* **Sourced, specific writing gets picked up more.** Researchers from Princeton, Georgia Tech, and IIT Delhi found that adding citations, quotes from credible sources, and statistics [raised a page's visibility in AI answers by up to 40%](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735). That is a lab result, not a promise for your site.
* **Each AI tool answers differently.** One agency's research found only 12% of citations overlap across [ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude](https://www.gtm8020.com/blog/aeo-agencies-dev-tools-api-companies). Check each one yourself.
* **Answers change between asks.** The same question can produce a different answer an hour later, so be wary of anyone who guarantees a ChatGPT ranking.
* **Search itself is shifting.** In 2024, Gartner predicted that [traditional search volume would fall 25% by 2026](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/will-search-engine-traffic-really-drop-25-by-2026-as-gartner-predicts/) as people move to AI chatbots. We have not seen a final measurement, so treat it as a prediction.
* **A plain-text docs file may help coding tools.** The HackerNoon checklist says Google does not use `llms.txt` and most of those files see no crawler traffic, but it is still worth shipping for developer docs.

Most AEO work for developer tools comes down to three jobs:

* **Clear docs, comparison pages, and tutorials on your own site**, with real numbers and sources. An agency can produce a lot of this quickly.
* **Honest answers where developers already talk**, like GitHub, Reddit, and community forums. This needs real credibility, which leans toward an in-house hire.
* **Regular checks on what each AI tool says about you**, then fixing the gaps. A junior teammate or an agency can own this.

So AEO does not push you hard in either direction. It makes the hybrid setup more attractive.

## Which option fits your stage?

These are our opinions, built on the cost data above.

### Pre-seed or seed

* **Budget:** About $2,000 to $5,000 a month for part-time help.
* **Best fit:** A solo DevRel consultant or a small content retainer. Jozu was at an early stage when we started working with them in October 2024. We began with developer content, community work, and events for their open-source project, KitOps, and kept going for just over a year. You can read the [Jozu case study](https://www.studio1hq.com/case-studies/jozu).
* **First 90 days:** Fix your quickstart, write five tutorials that answer real questions, and check what AI tools say about you.

### Series A

* **Budget:** One hire at about $195,000 a year, or a retainer of $8,000 to $15,000 a month.
* **Best fit:** An agency if you need output now. A first hire if you already have a community and a clear owner.
* **First 90 days:** Pick one number to move, such as how many new signups make their first successful API call. Check it every week and fix the step where most people drop off.

### Series B and later

* **Budget:** An in-house lead plus a retainer in the $8,000 to $15,000 range, more if several channels are involved.
* **Best fit:** The hybrid model.
* **First 90 days:** Set up reporting that ties DevRel work to signups and revenue, so the program survives the next budget round.

### Open source projects

* **Budget:** Usually small, often tied to a company sponsor.
* **Best fit:** Someone in-house who is already part of the project. Bring in an agency only for docs or specific pushes. We help open source teams with this kind of work. Our [Memori case study](https://www.studio1hq.com/case-studies/memori) covers a launch that went from 0 to 12,000+ GitHub stars, and our [Corsair case study](https://www.studio1hq.com/case-studies/corsair) covers developer experience, demos, and community growth.
* **First 90 days:** Cut the time it takes a new contributor to get a first pull request merged.

## What mistakes do teams make when choosing?

* **Hiring before defining success.** In the 2024 State of Developer Relations survey, [33.5% of practitioners could not state their pro](https://stateofdeveloperrelations.com/2024devrelreport)[gram budget, and another 17.8% had no set budget](https://www.skillsdirectory.com/skills/samber-devrel-strategy). A role with no goal and no budget is the first one cut.
* **Judging too early.** Reviewing a new hire at month three punishes the work that pays off at month nine.
* **Counting vanity numbers.** Followers and page views look good and prove little. Quickstart completions and signups are harder to fake.
* **Signing a long agency contract with no exit.** Ask what leaving after three months costs.
* **Giving an agency nobody to talk to.** Without an internal owner, the agency guesses at your product, and the work shows it.
* **Treating DevRel as a support desk.** Answering tickets matters, but it is not a strategy.

## What should you ask before you sign or hire?

If you are choosing an agency:

* **What proof do you have from a company like mine?** Ask for numbers you can check.
* **What is not in the monthly price?** Get the exclusions in writing.
* **Who owns the content and code when we stop?** Settle this before the first invoice.

If you are hiring a person:

* **Can they write and code?** Many are strong at only one.
* **Can they build a community and create content?** Developers expect good docs, tutorials, and quick replies on Discord or GitHub. Ask to see examples of each.
* **Have they built something developers still use?** A public track record beats a polished resume.
* **What budget and goals do they get in writing?** And does their manager understand the job?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a DevRel agency cost?

Pricing depends on scope, seniority, and allocated capacity. SaaSHero estimates DevRel agency retainers at $8,000–$28,000 per month, but this is a vendor estimate rather than an industry-wide average. Get a quote that specifies deliverables, exclusions, and contract terms.

### How much does it cost to hire a developer advocate?

Costs vary by location, experience, and responsibilities. In our illustrative US scenario, a $136,300 base salary becomes approximately $194,700 when applying the BLS private-industry compensation ratio. This is a budgeting example, not a verified average. Recruitment and program expenses are additional.

### Is a DevRel agency cheaper than hiring in-house?

It can be, depending on scope and total costs. Our illustrative $195,000 hiring scenario equals $16,250 per month before recruitment and program expenses. Compare this with the agency’s total cost over the same period, including excluded expenses and internal oversight.

### How long does it take to see results from DevRel?

Timelines depend on the product, starting point, and goals. Use the first 90 days to assess delivery, onboarding improvements, and initial developer responses. Set separate milestones for longer-term outcomes such as retention, community participation, and revenue contribution.

### Can an agency replace a Head of DevRel?

An agency or fractional leader can provide strategy, program leadership, and product-feedback coordination if those responsibilities are included in the engagement. Your company still needs an accountable decision-maker for priorities, budget, and internal access.

### Does DevRel help your product show up in ChatGPT and Claude answers?

Yes, indirectly. Clear docs, honest comparisons, sourced numbers, and real answers in developer communities give AI tools better material to quote. Nobody can promise a specific ranking, because AI answers change from one ask to the next.
