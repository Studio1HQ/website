**The short answer:** To launch a developer tool on Product Hunt, launch something people can try right away, schedule it for 12:01 AM Pacific time, and arrive with a listing that explains the product in seconds: a clear tagline, a demo video, and a maker comment that asks for feedback. Warm up your users and community in the weeks before, spend the 24 hours of launch day replying to every comment, and pair the launch with Hacker News, Reddit, X and a week of follow-up announcements. Product Hunt gives you one spike and a lasting listing. What you do around it decides whether that spike turns into users. That approach helped Webcrumbs finish #3 Product of the Day on its first launch ever.

![Product Hunt launch timeline in five stages: decide 4 to 6 weeks out, build the listing 2 to 3 weeks out, warm up in launch week, run launch day, and convert in the week after](/blog/uploads/product-hunt-launch-playbook/launch-timeline.svg "A Product Hunt launch, start to finish.")

## What Product Hunt can and can't do for a developer tool

Product Hunt is good at three things for a devtool: a concentrated burst of early adopters in a single day, honest feedback from people who try new products for fun, and a public listing with reviews and badges that buyers can find later. For Webcrumbs, the listing became a credibility surface it kept pointing to after launch day.

Standing out is harder than it used to be. Product Hunt now picks homepage launches on four criteria, [useful, novel, high craft and creative](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/9883485-product-hunt-featuring-guidelines), and says engagement is not the only factor. According to [Hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product-hunt-pulse), a third-party tracker, about 6% of launches were featured in 2025, and about 4% so far in 2026. Plan for a product that earns its place on merit, and treat the rest of this playbook as the work that gets it seen.

Product Hunt is also only one place developers look. In Stack Overflow's [2026 Developer Survey](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/), the most common ways developers look for answers were searching online (83%) and asking an AI agent (70%). A launch that leaves behind docs, comparisons and demos keeps showing up in those searches long after the badge.

## Step 1: Decide what you are launching (4 to 6 weeks out)

* **Launch something people can try.** A hosted product, a free tier, or an open-source repo with a working quickstart. A waitlist gives visitors nothing to do, and Hacker News bans sign-up pages from Show HN outright.
* **Pick the one thing that is new.** "Embeddable access sharing components," the tagline for Permit Share-If, tells a developer what it is in four words. A list of features does not.
* **Hunt it yourself.** Product Hunt [encourages self-hunting](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/before-launch) and says 79% of featured posts came from makers who hunted their own product. You don't need a famous hunter.
* **Choose and schedule the date.** You can [schedule a launch up to a month ahead](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/preparing-for-launch). Product Hunt has retired its old Coming Soon pages, and each product now gets a [forum thread](https://www.producthunt.com/p/general/changelog-august-28-no-more-coming-soon-and-a-couple-other-shiny-updates) that followers can watch, so start posting there once the product page exists.
* **Know the relaunch rule.** Product Hunt asks you to [wait at least two months](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/484934-can-i-relaunch-my-product) between launches of the same product or company, and only for a significant update. A redesign or a pricing change doesn't count.

## Step 2: Build a listing that explains the product in seconds (2 to 3 weeks out)

Visitors decide in a few seconds whether to click through. Every part of the listing should answer one question: what is this, and why should I care?

1. **Thumbnail:** square, 240 × 240. A GIF works if it is under 3 MB.
2. **Name and tagline:** the tagline has a 60-character limit. Say what the product does in a developer's words, not your positioning deck's.
3. **Gallery:** at least two images, 1270 × 760. Show the product, not slogans.
4. **Demo video:** a YouTube link that shows the product working in the first few seconds.
5. **Maker comment:** why you built it, what is new, who it is for, and a question that invites feedback. Product Hunt's launch guide says 70% of products that won Product of the Day, Week or Month had a first comment from the maker.
6. **Topics:** the categories developers browse, such as Developer Tools.

![Annotated Product Hunt listing mockup with six numbered parts: thumbnail, name and tagline, gallery, demo video, maker comment, and topics](/blog/uploads/product-hunt-launch-playbook/listing-anatomy.svg "Anatomy of a Product Hunt listing. Specs from Product Hunt's launch guide.")

All of these specs come from [Product Hunt's launch guide](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/preparing-for-launch).

Prepare what people see after they click, too: a landing page that matches the listing, a quickstart that works on the first try, and docs that answer the obvious questions. Product Hunt says there is [no penalty for linking directly to your product](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/launch-day-questions), so link straight to where a visitor can try it.

## Step 3: Warm up your community before the day

Launch day is too late to start telling people. In the weeks before:

* **Tell the people who already care.** Users, newsletter subscribers, your Discord or Slack community, and friends of the company should know the date and what you are launching.
* **Ask for feedback, not upvotes.** Product Hunt's rules are explicit: [ask for feedback, comments and help spreading the word, not upvotes](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/sharing-your-launch). Asking for upvotes can get a product unfeatured, and the [community guidelines](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/3615694-community-guidelines) rule out mass messaging and incentives. Product Hunt also [monitors sudden point swings and removes votes it doesn't consider genuine](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/11869098-how-does-product-hunt-ensure-fair-voting-and-prevent-spam-or-vote-manipulation).
* **Write a post for each channel.** A launch thread for X, a short post for LinkedIn, a problem-first post for the right subreddits, and a Show HN draft if the product fits.
* **Line up demos and use-case content.** Developers trust what they can run. A small demo project or a "how I built this" post gives the launch something to share besides the listing.

## Step 4: Run launch day, hour by hour

Product Hunt launches [run for 24 hours](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/479557-how-to-post-a-product) from 12:01 AM Pacific time. Here is how the day breaks down.

![Launch day timeline in Pacific time: go live at 12:01 AM, Europe and India carry the early hours, the US wakes up from 6 to 10 AM, a mid-day update in the afternoon, and the day closes at 11:59 PM](/blog/uploads/product-hunt-launch-playbook/launch-day.svg "Launch day, hour by hour.")

* **12:01 AM PT:** go live, post the maker comment, and tell your team.
* **The first hours:** it is morning in Europe and afternoon in India, so supporters in those time zones carry the early hours.
* **6 to 10 AM PT:** the US wakes up. Post your launch thread on X and LinkedIn, email your users, and share in your community.
* **All day:** reply to every comment within minutes. The leaderboard moves on [upvotes, comments and time since posting](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/how-product-hunt-works), and a fast, useful reply turns a passer-by into a user.
* **Afternoon:** post an update with what people asked, what you fixed, and what is next.
* **11:59 PM PT:** the day closes. Product of the Day, Week and Month go to the launches with the most [points, which combine upvotes with meaningful engagement like comments and shares](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/11751186-product-of-the-day-week-month).

Permit.io's own [write-up of its 2023 launch](https://permit.io/blog/producthunt-howto), which finished #1 of the day, shows what a strong day can deliver: 300+ upvotes in the first two hours, 1.3K landing page visits in 24 hours, a signup spike of about 500%, and seven sales meetings booked within a day.

## Step 5: Go beyond Product Hunt with Hacker News and a launch week

**Show HN.** Hacker News is often a better fit for open-source and API products, because readers can try them on the spot. The [Show HN rules](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) are strict: it must be something people can play with, ideally without a signup. Landing pages, fundraisers and blog posts don't qualify, and you shouldn't ask friends to upvote or comment. Set expectations, too: an [analysis of 188,000 Show HN posts](https://danfking.github.io/blog/2026/04/23/show-hn-by-the-numbers/) found the median post gets 2 points, and 50 points puts a post in the top 6%.

How you frame the post matters as much as where you post it. Memori's plain Show HN post earned 17 points. A later post framed around a debate developers were already having, ["Everyone's trying vectors and graphs for AI memory. We went back to SQL"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45329322), reached 136 points and 63 comments.

**Launch week.** Instead of betting everything on one day, many developer companies now ship one announcement a day for a week. Supabase made this popular with its first [Launch Week in March 2021](https://supabase.com/blog/2021/11/26/supabase-how-we-launch), Cloudflare's [2026 Birthday Week](https://blog.cloudflare.com/birthday-week-2026-wrap-up/) shipped 46 announcements, and Permit.io ran [its own launch week](https://www.permit.io/blog/launch-week-1-devex) in October 2024. A launch week gives every channel a fresh reason to share, and gives Product Hunt, Hacker News and Reddit a day each.

![Example launch week across five days: Hacker News, Reddit, X and LinkedIn, Product Hunt, then daily.dev and Dev.to, with daily issue replies, docs fixes, and small releases](/blog/uploads/how-to-get-github-stars-open-source-launch/launch-week.svg "One way to spread the channels across a week.")

## Step 6: Turn the spike into users

The badge fades in a day. What lasts is what you do next:

* **Thank people publicly** and share a short recap of what you learned and what you are shipping next.
* **Fix what launch day found.** Every confused question in the comments is a docs fix or an onboarding change.
* **Follow up with commenters** who asked about their use case. They are the warmest leads a launch brings in.
* **Publish content that keeps working:** comparison pages, tutorials and "how to" posts that match what developers search for. For Webcrumbs, "v0 alternative" comparison content kept bringing in developers long after launch week.
* **Add the badge** to your site and README if you earned one.

## FAQ

### What time should I launch on Product Hunt?

At 12:01 AM Pacific time. Product Hunt's day runs for 24 hours in Pacific time, so launching at the start gives you the whole day.

### Should I hunt my own product?

Yes. Product Hunt encourages self-hunting, and says 79% of featured posts came from makers who hunted their own product.

### Can I ask people to upvote my launch?

No. Ask for feedback, comments and help spreading the word. Product Hunt can unfeature products that ask for upvotes, and it removes votes it doesn't consider genuine.

### Is Product Hunt worth it for developer tools?

Yes, as one part of a launch. It brings a day of early adopters and a listing that lasts. Pair it with Hacker News, Reddit, X and follow-up content, because developers also find tools through search and AI assistants.

### Should I launch on Product Hunt or Hacker News first?

Use Product Hunt for a hosted product or a site people can visit, and Show HN for something developers can try directly, such as an open-source repo. Many teams do both in the same launch week.

### How often can I relaunch on Product Hunt?

Product Hunt asks for at least two months between launches of the same product or company, and only for a significant update.
